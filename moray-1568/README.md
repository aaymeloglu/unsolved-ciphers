# Regent Moray to John Wood, 13 July 1568: the cipher postscript

**Status: substantially read, 18 September 2026.** 119 of 134 glyphs S (cryptanalysis with a
permutation control), 14 M, 1 I. No key survives and no contemporary decipherment is known, so
there are no H or C tokens: this is a cryptanalytic result. Not the first edition of the letter's
clear text, which Bain does not calendar; we have found no printed reading of the postscript.

## The document

British Library, Add MS 32091 ff. 213–214. James Stewart, Earl of Moray, Regent of Scotland,
to "Maister Johne" Wood, his secretary then at Elizabeth's court, Edinburgh, 13 July 1568,
signed "James Regent"; endorsed on f. 214v "18 July 1568 … my Lord Regent to Mr Jhone Wod …
attending at the court in England". The body is in clear Scots, about the coming inquiry into
Mary's case, the sending of commissioners, and the Borders ("slauchter dalie … the mater is
like anewch to fall to sic ane inconvenient"). The cipher is a four-line postscript between the
date line and the signature. DECODE lists the page as
[R8345](https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/8345), status non-decrypted, with Satoshi
Tomokiyo's 2024 transcription attached; his
[unsolved list](https://cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/unsolved.htm) carries it as "Moray-Wood
Cipher (1568)" with the note "although the cipher seems simple, the short ciphertext is not
deciphered". DECODE's date of 1558 for the record is wrong. The images are the British
Library's and are not redistributed here.

## The reading

Mechanical output of [`key.json`](key.json) over [`transcription.txt`](transcription.txt)
([`reading.txt`](reading.txt), regenerated by `verify.py`); `|` marks a gap the scribe left on
the page, `[..]` a word-sign, `(..)` a glyph graded M or I:

```
L1: [the] | sv(f)eringof[Q]remaneinca(r)l(y)l(r) | [and] | sending | ha(y)m | of[the]lord
L2: fleminghesdonegreitevilgif[the][Q2] | cummis | haomthair
L3: la(k)(k)is | namairto | s(p)il[the] | haille(t) | oure | freindis
L4: (k)(e)a(?)ois
```

Divided into words:

> The suffering of [Q] remane in Carlyle, and sending haym of the Lord Fleming, hes done greit
> evil. Gif the [Q2] cummis haom thair, la(tt)is na mair to s(p)il the haill e(t) oure freindis
> (te)a(?)ois.

> The allowing of [the Queen] to remain in Carlisle, and the sending home of Lord Fleming, has
> done great harm. If the [Queen] comes home there, [she/it] lets nothing more [stand] to ruin the
> whole […] our friends […].

Mary was moved from Carlisle to Bolton on 13–15 July 1568, the date of the letter. Lord Fleming,
sent to Elizabeth with Herries in June, was back in Scotland in arms by 21 August (Bain, CSP
Scotland ii, no. 775). The two person-signs are read as the Queen from context only.

## The cipher

A simple substitution with a few homophones and a few word-signs, written with word spacing.
Thirty-three glyph shapes: 27 letter-glyphs for 21 plaintext letters (two glyphs each for f, i,
m, o, r; none needed for b, k, q, w, x, z in this text), a boxy sign that stands alone at the
start and before "lord", "[Q2]" and "haill" and reads *the*, a dotted o that stands alone
between two clauses and is read *and*, and two barred signs (a 4 and an E) for a person or
party. The glyph repertoire (x-forms, 3-forms, cross-T forms, triangles) is the one used by the
Scottish and English secretariats of the 1560s, and is shared with the John Wood → Cecil line
of 6 September 1568 on the decipherer's specimen sheet BL Add MS 4136 f. 33 (DECODE R2989),
whose plaintext Bain prints. That line is in a different key: fixing its twelve letter values on
this text scores no better than shuffled assignments (`cribtest`: 2 of 12 shuffles beat it), and
Tomokiyo's Elizabethan ciphers page calls it "a different, unknown cipher". The Mary–Hamilton
and Throckmorton–Moray alphabets attached to DECODE R8347/R8348 do not contain this text's
glyphs either.

## Method, and what the controls say

1. **Transcription from the page.** Tomokiyo's transcription conflates two glyphs under one id
   (the boxy *the*-sign and the tailed o that reads m) and reads the same f-barred glyph once
   as s and once as f. Ours was made glyph by glyph from the DECODE image with connected-
   component sheets; gaps between glyph groups were measured (`gaps`) and the sixteen chunks
   they delimit are kept in the transcription.
2. **Solver.** [`solver.py`](solver.py): simulated annealing over glyph→letter maps, scored by
   the best dictionary segmentation of each chunk (word unigram log-probabilities from a Scots
   corpus, a character 4-gram with a penalty for words outside it, a penalty of 20 per shared
   letter), word-signs held as breaks.
3. **How the key was found.** Unconstrained runs do not converge at 134 glyphs. One run produced
   *sending*, *freindis* and *oure* under one map; those eleven values were held and the rest
   re-annealed, which gave *remane in*, *na mair to*, *hes done greit evil gif*, *haill*,
   *thair*; then *cummis* and *Fleming* once the two p-shapes were separated on the glyph sheet;
   then *Carlyle* and *haym* once the slashed 3-shape was separated from the plain one. Bain's
   own spellings "Carlyle", "haym" and "cummis" are in the corpus.
4. **Matched controls.** Four 128-letter Scots passages from the corpus were enciphered with
   random keys of the same design (23 letters, four homophones, two word-signs, word spacing)
   and given to the annealer unconstrained, four restarts each. Token-weighted key recovery:
   0.99, 0.35, 0.09, and fragments only ("schort", "cum") on the fourth. One control in four
   reads; the target behaved like the other three. The reading therefore rests on the
   hand-iterated fixes, not on convergence, and its statistical warrant is the permutation
   test.
5. **Permutation test.** The dictionary-segmentation score of the text under the key, against
   1,000 keys that shuffle the letter values among the letter-glyphs: **z = 17.3, p < 0.001**
   with cipherkit's `sco` corpus (Diurnal of Occurrents, Knox); z = 13.1 against 300 shuffles
   with the CSP-based corpus used during the campaign (`verify.py --z`).

## Open points (the 14 M glyphs and the I)

- **Carlyle** (`4 A c a Z5 a c`): the two c-shapes have to be r and e for the name and are the
  same shape; the last is read as a slip for e. `Z5` is y (Bain: "Carlyle", "haym") or i
  ("Carlile", "haim").
- **suffering** (`Zh P g x o Ab xd Tb`): `P` is the u/v glyph; the single curl `g` stands for
  f or ff.
- **haom thair**: the glyphs are in the order h-a-o-m; read as *hame* (home) with a transposed
  letter, or an unrecognised spelling.
- **la(tt)is**: a doubled glyph `Zz`. In a 190,000-form lexicon built from the Register of the
  Privy Council i–ii, Bain i–ii and ix, Pitscottie and Knox, *lattis* (lets) has 8 attestations
  and *lakkis* none; *laikis* 2. `Zz` opens the last word too.
- **s(p)il**: `s` occurs once; *spil* (spoil, ruin) by sense.
- **haill e(t)**: `t` occurs once; the two letters after *haill* are not resolved.
- **Line 4** `(t)(e)a(?)ois`: one word of seven glyphs, two of them singletons (`x3`, `Xs`);
  unread. No seven-letter form `??a?ois` occurs in the 190,000-form lexicon (only OCR debris of
  *François*), and a brute-force over the three free glyphs finds nothing. If the group is two
  words, the last four glyphs read *dois* (does, 134 attestations) with `Xs` a d-homophone, and
  the first three `t?a` (*twa*?). The letters b, k, q, w, x, z are otherwise unused in the
  text, so `x3` and `Xs` are likely among them. The Dictionary of the Older Scottish Tongue
  headword search returns no results for `??a?ois`, `t?a?ois`, `twa?ois` or `??a?oys`
  (dsl.ac.uk, 18 September 2026); its 6–8-letter *-ois* headword forms are *purpois*,
  *propois*, *suppois*, *repois*, *impois*, *inclois*, *unclois*, *undois* and the like, none of
  which fits. Possibly a name. The idiom *cummis hame thair*
  occurs 43 times in the Register of the Privy Council ii.
- The person-signs `4̄` and `Ē`, and the lone dotted o read as *and*.

## Failure log

14 September 2026: n-gram hill climbing and annealing on Tomokiyo's transcription, with and
without nomenclator slots, homophone penalties 10–30, 24 restarts: no two seeds agree.
16 September: Wood → Cecil crib (35 letters, Bain no. 804) mapped onto Tomokiyo's ids, five
to eight letters fixed, six restarts: no convergence. 18 September: page image obtained; the
crib rejected by shuffle test; word gaps read off the page; dictionary-segmentation annealer
with break signs; the key found as above.
