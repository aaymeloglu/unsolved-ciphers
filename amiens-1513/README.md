# Roberto Acciaiuoli at Amiens, 23 October 1513

**Partial reading, 20 November 2026.** Louis XII has ordered Monsieur de Bourbon into Burgundy. Acciaiuoli discusses how news of a defeat changed a plan, reports a bitter exchange about responsibility for endangering the kingdom, follows the English embarkation and ends with the French arrangements for sending prelates to Rome. Several clauses remain unread, including ones that affect who said what.

The manuscript is Florence, Archivio di Stato, Mediceo avanti il Principato, filza LXXII, doc. 192, old c. 234. Both faces are 1264 × 1677 pixels at the archive's native resolution. Its heading is plural, *Magnifici viri*, and its subscription names Robertus Acciarolus. The cipher occupies 22 lines on the recto and 19 on the verso, counting the last signs on the line where ordinary handwriting resumes. [Recto](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=79574) · [Verso](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=79575)

In the clear closing Acciaiuoli commends himself to the recipients and dates the letter *Ex Amiens, die xxiii Octobris M.D.XIII*. This supplies the date independently of the cipher.

## How the key was identified

Brice Dudout had already reconstructed a Florentine syllabary for Francesco Pandolfini’s letters from the French court in 1514–15. Many of those values also read passages in the 1513 letter. Dudout describes the reconstructed key as partial. In particular, the 2-like sign in *Borbona* takes the b reading from context. A consonant sign usually takes its following vowel from a superscript, with 1–5 standing for **u, o, i, e, a**. There are also independent vowels, double consonants, whole words, and integers used as code words. The symbol shaped like a 7 and the short r-shaped sign must be distinguished. The same problem occurs with the signs for r and g, and with 5-shaped m and qu. [Dudout’s key and source pairs](https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver/tree/main/targets/pandolfini1514)

Doc. 190 is a letter from the same day to a singular addressee. Doc. 191 starts by repeating its clear account of the arrival of Sanseverino on the evening of the 20th. Across the transition into cipher, the letter groups expand to *le · le · tt · e · re · di*, and the clear copy has *Le lettere di …*. In the interior, doc. 190r line 11 produces portions of *de secreti*, *amico di* and *confusione* in the order present in 191. The first-pass reading of that line also produces errors between the matches. This is a local cross-check. No blind whole-letter accuracy rate was measured. [Doc. 190r](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=79570) · [Doc. 191r](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=79572)

On doc. 192, the chain for *monsignor di Borbona* expands as **mo · n · si · g · no · r | di | bo · r · bo · na**. The opening and verso give a second internal check. The sign sequence for *distribui-* occurs with two endings, first *distribuire le guernigione* (R2), then *distribuito el passaggio loro* (V4). *Guernigione* itself recurs at R22, where the assigned troops have still to depart.

**Numerals.** I read an unmarked 8 before the syllables for *cento lance*, and 10 before the syllables for *mila*. Their numerical use is an interpretation. An unmarked 8 can elsewhere stand for a consonant. In the later Pandolfini material 4 means the French king or his title *Cristianissimo*, 6 refers to England, and 20 to the Swiss. Dudout tentatively identifies 22 as *lanzichenech*. Occurrences of 22 after *mila* in both R3 and V7 support a troop category. On the second face, the unmarked 2 and 5 precede syllabically written *mila* and *cento*. The following 14 precedes *mila 22*. This is the same mixed notation as on R2–3. I treat both sets of counts as provisional. [Key table](https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver/blob/main/targets/pandolfini1514/key_v6.json) · [Discussion of code 22](https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver/blob/main/targets/pandolfini1514/NOTES.md)

## Documentary checks

Marino Sanudo’s *Diarii*, vol. XVII, cols. 321–324, records a separate report of the king assigning Bourbon to Burgundy with **900 men-at-arms and 10,000 infantry**, subsequently described as Landsknechte. This confirms the commander, destination and infantry component. The letter’s apparent 800 lances and Sanudo’s 900 men-at-arms must be kept separate. Another report in the same volume gives 1,500 lances and 15,000 Landsknechte. The volume also records that news of a defeat changed French plans to send troops to Italy, a report that the people of Tournai had sent to the French king to explain their conduct, and news of prelates to be sent to Rome. These reports cannot identify a missing cipher name on their own. The surviving 5 September dispatch printed under Acciaiuoli’s name is a different letter. [Sanudo, vol. XVII](https://archive.org/details/idiariidimarinos17sanu)

The photographic index to Gabbrielli’s historical compilation lists **no. 120, Acciaioli Roberto, 1512, 1513, 1514**. I read the entry on the index image. The online Yale reel contains volume I, with numbered image files through 58-51 and three index images. The row identifies a specific historical key to compare. I did not obtain its table or verify its code numbers. [Gabbrielli’s index, image 2](https://dataverse.yale.edu/api/access/datafile/7983) · [Yale reel inventory](https://doi.org/10.60600/YU/XKVOWE)

## Working evidence

- [First-pass sign transcription](192-first-pass.txt) covers all cipher lines on both faces. Its provisional shape labels include misread superscripts and ambiguous abbreviations. The [first-pass decoder](first_pass.py) reproduces the early noisy output when given the transcription filename.
- [Normalized sign alignments](passages.tsv) record the readings selected after enlargement and linguistic review. Running [the verifier](verify.py) prints the expansions and checks their spelling against the edited text, folding u/v. Their number does not measure independent accuracy or coverage. They use 2b for the b value of the 2-like sign, P? for an inferred p, and # before literal numerals. A canonical label can incorporate an editorial choice between similar signs.
- [Doc. 190, first-pass transcription](190-first-pass.txt) covers both faces of the comparison letter. The [interior test transcription](190-interior.txt) and [additional samples](190-checks.txt) preserve the earlier spot checks. A character model and word-list search used the repository’s Italian corpus and Ridolfi comparison texts. The word search also included Sanudo and candidate terms such as *Borbona*. Their suggestions produced many false word breaks and carry no independent evidentiary weight.
- The six native public source images (Florence viewer IDs 79570–79575) were inspected, including the back of 191. The clear letter is on its front. Comparison scans in docs. 189 and 193–197 yielded no clear copy of 192 in the material examined. Its unresolved passages include the Martelli report, several referents of the royal conversation, the attribution of the second troop count, and the detailed terms in the Roman negotiations.


## Reproducing the readings

From the repository root, run:

```sh
python3 amiens-1513/first_pass.py 192-first-pass.txt
python3 amiens-1513/verify.py
```

The first command prints the literal output for the 41 manuscript lines. The second checks it against the [saved first pass](192-first-pass-decode.txt) and expands each reviewed sign sequence beside its Italian reading. The first-pass labels and the normalized passage labels use different conventions. The verifier checks the transcribed data and its expansions. Reading a given shape or distinguishing *b* from *c* in context still requires the manuscript. The ellipses have uncertain extent, so a whole-letter word percentage and a manuscript-wide count by evidence grade remain open.

A second model checked the recto military order (R2–3) and the closing passage on peace in Christendom (V17–19) against enlarged manuscript strips and the working key, and compared the troop figures with Sanudo. Other passages await independent review.
