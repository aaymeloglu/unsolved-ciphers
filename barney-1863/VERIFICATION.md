# Barney to Mallory, 19 March 1863: dictionary code, independent verification (2026-09-13)

**Status: solved by Reddit user offgramercy, r/codes, ~mid-August 2026** (post "I think I cracked a Confederate Navy cipher (Barney to Mallory, 1863) using a dictionary from 1850"). Tomokiyo's Cryptiana unsolved list (updated 2026-09-06) still shows it open. Verified here independently against the scan.

**Key:** Webster, *A Primary School Pronouncing Dictionary of the English Language, abridged from the American Dictionary* (1850), Harvard copy, HathiTrust hvd.32044086661170. Three columns per page. HathiTrust seq = printed page + 38.

**Rule:** (page)-column-N, where N is the ordinal of the headword within the column, counting each entry of a brace-paired variant separately (e.g. Bas-tin-ade'/Bas-ti-na'do count as two). Not a printed line number.

**Source text:** ORN ser. 1 vol. 20 p. 805 (Internet Archive officialrecordso0020unse). Key named in Barney to Mallory, 23 Feb 1863, ORN vol. 19 ("a copy of Webster's Dictionary for the purpose designated").

17 of 18 groups exact; 113-3-85 gives GUN at 8.

**Plaintext (ORN wording, decoded words in caps):**
"In my last of 9th instant by Lieutenant Warley I reported that General Magruder proposed to PLACE the SHIP GUN[S] in BATTERY ASHORE. I am officially informed that OBSTRUCTION will prevent EGRESS from HARBOR. As my previous suggestions are thus defeated, I presume the VESSEL will not be kept in COMMISSION, SHE being entirely useless. I beg leave respectfully to suggest that being so near MAT-a-MORASS [Matamoras] the OFFICER[s] might be SENT ABROAD. ... P.S. What shall be done with the MEN sent from Richmond in case the above suggestion is carried out? Some might, if practicable, be sent ABROAD."
