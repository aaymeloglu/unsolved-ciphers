### Shapes, family by family

**d vs y: bowl plus ascender** (`compare_dy.png`: d at L1 1440 and L10 2130; y at L4 1460, L9 2020, L3 840, L2 745, L8 1395)
- **d (true d, plain q).** A small c-shaped bowl with a **thin, separate, near-vertical ascender** standing to its right, often slightly detached from the bowl.
  - It is light and upright, with no loop and no leftward lean.
  - It is rare: 2 in the covered text, both in "q-" (venticinque, quei). One more true d is certain after the clear (L12 x~2560, "que-sta").
- **y (plain l).** The bowl is heavier and the ascender **rises from the bowl's right side and leans or curls left** at the top. It often reads as a looped "d" or a cursive "al".
  - A hairline usually runs from its foot into the next sign.
  - It is common (31): lo, la, il, dal, del, l'animo, ultima, volunta, egli.
- Rule of thumb:
  - Ascender upright, thin and not joined to the bowl: d.
  - Ascender part of the bowl's stroke and leaning back: y.
  - Faint or damaged top: `{y/d}`, expecting y about 5:1.
- One sign does not fit the rule: L12 x~1230, right before L14. The language wants y ("li Venetiani"), but the shape is a d with a heavy vertical ascender. It is transcribed `{d/y}`.

**q, g, z, t: signs with descenders** (`compare_qgzt.png`: q at L1 1565, L2 1415; z at L1 695, L3 422; g at L2 1195, L2 1305; t at L1 1665, L9 1960)
- **q (plain d).** An open or closed bowl with a **long, straight descender** that ends blunt or in a slight hook. There is no lower loop.
- **z (plain m).** A small closed **numeral-9 bowl** at x-height. Its tail curves **down and to the left in a single stroke**, ending in a heavy blob or foot well left of the bowl. It is shorter than q's descender and never loops back up.
- **g (plain t).** A taller bowl, often with a heavy wedge on the top right. The descender **forms a closed or nearly closed lower loop**, like a figure 8 or a modern g. This loop is what separates g from z.
- **t (plain g).** A **cross-form sign**: an upright with a long crossbar, standing on the baseline, with no descender loop. It looks like a dagger or a small "t" or "f".
  - It is not f (plain s), which has a long descender below the baseline.
  - It is not x (plain i), whose strokes are both diagonal.
  - Examples: L1 1665 (giugno), L9 1960 (egli), L12 1880 (grandemente), L13 650 (lega).
- The digit 9 in 19, 39 and 69 has the same shape as z. Read it as a digit only when it stands next to another digit.
- The 8 in 18 can have a descending lower loop like g (L3 x~1565). The tell is that it follows a 1.

**c vs e: open c-shapes** (`compare_ce.png`: c at L9 1270, L4 1532, L10 1800; e at L9 600, 2330, 2520; L6 730)
- **c (plain p).** A plain open c, usually with a heavy top that curls slightly in, and **no** inner stroke.
- **Cipher e (plain r)** takes four forms, and three of them do not look like e:
  1. An ordinary looped e (e_1).
  2. A **c-shape with a small inner tick or hook** (e-hook_1-2). Under magnification at L9 2330 and 2520 it shows a closed little eye at the top.
  3. An **accented e** with a dot, tick or separate stroke above. It can be taken for i (e-accent_1-2, also L10 1180).
  4. A **final e** whose exit rises in a loop, so the sign looks like a small d or y (e-final_1-2: L5 1822 "mandar", L7 1745 "sospettar").
- The final e is the one most easily confused with d. It is the last letter of a word, has no separate bowl before it, and its loop rises from the e's own exit stroke. L14 x~575 ("saper") is probably this form and is transcribed `{e/d}`.
- **Where shape alone could not decide:**
  - At 3.5x, L6 x~730 ("loro", needs e) shows a faint inner mark.
  - L4 x~1532 ("po", needs c) shows none.
  - Both were decided by the clear and are marked as such.
  - Expect a tick to be lost in faint ink, so an open c-shape is `{c/e}` unless the tick is visible.

**s vs f: s-shapes**
- **f (plain s).** A long s: an f with a crossbar and a full descender, often a tall ascender too.
  - In the ligature `f x` ("si"), the x's diagonal crosses the f's bar, so the pair looks like one crossed sign (fx_1, fx_2).
- **s (plain f).** A **short, round s** sitting on the baseline, with no descender (s_1 fatto, s_2 fatto, s_3 Ferrara).
  - It has the same shape as the digit 5, which appears only in 45 and 53.
  - Lumping the long f with the short s merges plain s into plain f. That merge may explain "fara" in L10 (section 3).

**Other letters**
- **n (plain a).** A two-minim n with a rounded arch.
- **u (plain h).** Open at the top and joined at the bottom. It is rare: ha, hanno, ho, homo.
- **a (plain n).** An a with a closed bowl and a short upright that stays at x-height.
  - If the upright rises above x-height, the sign moves toward d or y. L10 x~790 ("ritorno") is transcribed `{a/d}`.
- **r (plain e).** An italic r with a shoulder. It sometimes takes a v-like form (r-v_1, L8 752).
  - Do not read the v-form as u.
- **h (plain u/v).** A tall looped ascender over an n-like body.
  - Plain v is always written with h: "voluntà" is written h b y h a g n, and the same holds for "volta" and "voce".
  - "Venetia" is written h x a r t x n, which reads "uinegia" (Vinegia).
- **b (plain o).** A looped ascender over a round body. **The body is the same shape as the digit 6.**
  - In "hanno" (u n 16 b), the b after 16 looks like a third digit.
  - Read a 6-shape as a digit only directly after a 1, or directly before a 4 or 9.
- **p (plain c).** A p with a long straight descender and an open bowl: ca(valcanti), (per)che, cosi.
  - Cipher c and cipher p carry mirrored plain values (p and c), which makes them easy to swap mentally.
  - Check the descender, not the bowl.
- **o (plain b).** A small round o without an ascender. It occurs once (L1, "bartholo").
  - It is distinct from the smaller raised ring of the codes 2o, 3o and 4o.
- **x (plain i).** A St Andrew's cross. It ligatures with a preceding f or q by crossing their bars.

Points for a fresh page:

- **L43 vs L 43** (`compare_L.png`). In L3 and L8 the 43 is small, raised and tucked against the L's foot. In L7 the 43 is the same height as the L's bowl-letters, sits on the baseline, and is separated from the L.
  - The digit shapes are the same, so the distinction rests on size and position alone. Two cases of the superscript form and one of the full-size form are a thin basis. Treat the rule as provisional.
  - The same caution applies to plain 14 (ll) vs L14 (Venetiani).
- **The 4-family.** 4o and 45 start with the same open 4 with a single crossbar. The q x ligature looks like that 4 with a double bar and a trailing dot.
  - Tell them apart by the bar and by what follows: a ring for 4o, a 5 for 45.
- **3o looks like "zo"; 2 looks like z.**
  - The 3 in 3o, 53, 73 and 43 has a z-like descending tail.
  - The 2 in 2o, 24 and 27 is a small z-like stroke on the baseline with no tail.
- **A dotted minim before a digit is the digit 1**, not cipher i. Cipher i (plain x) does not occur.

## 3. What does not fit the swap, and where the cipher's wording differs

**Nothing in the covered text needs a letter value outside the swap.** The one place where the signs as written give a different word from the clear is **L10 "s n e n" = "fara"**, where the clear has "sarà". That is either an encipherer's s/f slip or a long f that I have misread as a short s.

Wording differences (the key is unaffected):

- L1: two nulls, 27 and 24.
- L3-4: "andatsa | sua". The a of -ata is missing, and "sua" is begun at the margin and rewritten in L4.
- L4: "mi" for m'; "po" for può (also L9).
- L5: "di mandar" for "mandare".
- L7: "men da" for "meno da"; "sospettar"; "a imperiali" with no "gli".
- L8: "si e" for s'è; "data" for "dato"; "il" for 'l.
- L9: "partire" for "partir"; "infin" for "sin"; "ritornato" for "tornato".
- L10: "il ritorno suo" for "il suo ritorno".
- L11: "si risolue r{x/n} 16 b" ("si risolueranno") for "debban risolversi". The `{x/n}` sign is read as n on language alone.
