## General observations

- **h** occurs in three shapes: the looped h of glyphs.md; the long sloping form (stem from above x-height to below the baseline, arch on the right), which in this hand often looks like `f r`, `f c` or `f n`; and a short form (thin stem from upper right with a small hook at the left, e.g. L16 950, L20 1440, L21 930). I wrote h whenever the arch and the stem are one stroke and wrote a separate r only where a separate r-flag stands after the arch. Where the boundary is unclear it is noted per line. Criterion for long h versus f + next sign: a crossbar through the stem means f followed by a separate sign (L18 1850 and 2410 `f c`, L20 2440 `f p`); a stem with no crossbar whose arch comes off it means h (L3 750, L6 1600, L13 1020, L22 1640).
- **Four-downstroke clusters** were split by where the joins fall: u n where the first two strokes join at the foot (L18 640, L24 1730), m where a lead-in is followed by three arches joined at the top (L17 1880, L21 1010). Either could be the other.
- **The `e`+ascender sign** (`{e/y}`, L7 2205, L13 2160, L17 940, L20 2150): a looped e-bowl with an inner eye whose exit rises into a tall ascender with a foot. glyphs.md's "final e" form, or y written with an e-like bowl. It recurs, so a later reader should check it against the clear sections of the siblings.
- **Open-topped g** (L9 700, L10 1500, L25 650, L26 660, L27 820): an upstroke into an open bowl whose descender loops back closed. Written g throughout, because the closed lower loop is the g/z discriminator in glyphs.md. Only L15 1880, where the loop is a faint hairline, keeps `{g/z}`.
- **Wedge-topped descender** (`{q/1}`, L14 2610, L23 1050, L24 770): a heavy wedge at top left over a long straight descender, with no visible bowl. A q whose bowl is collapsed, or a 1 with a long tail.
- **Line-end group** (L25 2580, L26 2600): a minim, then a q/open-4 shape with an x-like stroke through and after its bar (with a trailing dot in L25). Compared side by side with `glyphs/qx_1.png`, `4o_1.png` and the L23 2400 `4o`: it matches the q x ligature, not 4o (no ring), so it is written `1 {q/4} x`. The minim could instead be part of the ligature or an i.
- **Bare 8** with no minim before it: L23 1650, L24 590, L25 1180.
- **Groups:** `12` at L14 1960-2010 (a doubling sign of section 6) and `64` at L27 1180 (section 2). `k` appears twice (L14 1450, L17 1770).
- **Undotted minims written `i`** where no digit follows (L3 2070 written `1`; L6 2090, L8 1590, L10 1210, L16 1240, L21 2440, L22 1340 written `i`). Cipher i (plain x) is said to be rare, so some of these may be the digit 1 or part of a neighbouring sign.
- **Bare L** (full size, followed by a letter) occurs at L1, L3 (`L 53`), L5, L7, L8 (`L10` raised, then `L 35`), L11, L17 x2, L18, L25 x2, L26. Only L8 has a raised number (`L10`).

- **Gaps:** one `|` on the page (L11 x~1995-2045, by eye). A column-ink projection of each band's x-height zone (threshold 100) found no interior gap of 45 px or more on any line, L11 included (strokes from the neighbouring lines run through it), so the L11 `|` is a weak call.
- **Not checked:** I did not do a blind re-read across the `halves/` seams to catch dropped or doubled signs at my own crop seams (x~980, 1540, 2100).
- **Unplaced interlinear sign:** a small cross-form t with a double crossbar between lines 17 and 18 at x~2210-2230, with no caret. It is in neither line of the transcription. It may be an insertion for line 18 after the L at x~2160.
