# Cardinal Ercole Gonzaga to Ferrante Gonzaga, two cipher letters of 1543–44

**Partial: substantially deciphered, with a continuous reading of both letters, 24 November 2026.** The seven cipher pages hold 7,890 signs and decode to 2,138 words. Of these, 1,958 (91.6%) are read as the signs stand, 42 are read with a letter supplied in brackets, 28 are read around a doubtful or unvalued sign, and 110 (5.1%) are not read. That is short of the 95% of sure words that Cyphersolver asks before a document counts as read. The counts come from `check_readme.py`, which also verifies the Italian below against the edition. I have not searched the printed literature on Ercole Gonzaga for these letters, so I do not know whether they have been read before.

The letters are Modena, Biblioteca Estense Universitaria, Autografoteca Campori, Lettera G, Gonzaga, Ercole (cardinale), sottofascicolo III, cc. 77–79 and cc. 174–175 (Cyphersolver catalogue no. 1556, where they are undated). [Letter A, cc. 77–79](https://lodovico.medialibrary.it/media/schedadl.aspx?id=6b71f191-e706-4ecd-91e0-21b35d5cf086) · [Letter B, cc. 174–175](https://lodovico.medialibrary.it/media/schedadl.aspx?id=9a179607-eab4-43e9-a552-46623faf7ccd). The images are not in this repository.

- **Letter A** (cc. 78r–79r, 71 cipher lines) is enclosed in a covering page in the cardinal's own hand (c. 77r) and addressed to Ferrante as viceroy and captain general of His Majesty. The note of receipt on the outside reads *Car.le di Mant.a 4 di Maggio* with the year figures 44 raised above it. The cardinal defends the orders he gave for Casale after a French victory, sets out his fear of losing either Monferrato or Mantua while the duke is a minor, and reports from Mirandola, where the count and Pietro Strozzi say they have six thousand men.
- **Letter B** (cc. 174r–175v, 103 cipher lines) has no address, date or docket, and breaks off in mid-sentence at the foot of c. 175v. The cardinal asks Ferrante to tell him whether his informant on the Pope's dealings with France is being duped, and passes on what the man reports: the Pope's complaint to the Emperor about the Diet, the Lutherans and the promises of Busseto, the Emperor's answer, the Pope's sending both letters to the King of France, and Farnese's errand about Milan.

## Dates

The catalogue files both letters under 1545 only as a sort year. Letter A is dated by its docket. A reader who was shown only a crop of the docket, with no expected date, read the day as 4 and the month as Maggio with confidence, and the raised year as 54, sure of the second figure and less sure of the first. The dockets of the sibling letters cc. 102–105 and 106–110 are laid out the same way in what looks like the same hand, whose covering letters are dated 4 and 10 July 1544 in clear, and there the raised year has the same first figure in the same place. I read it as 44. The address to Ferrante as viceroy agrees with that.

Letter B is dated only by what it says, and this part is my inference from the general chronology, not checked against the literature: Ferrante has left for England, Farnese has gone on his legation, and the Diet is in prospect, which fits the winter of 1543–44. It was therefore written some months before letter A.

The editors' notes in `edition/` were written while the letters were still filed under 1545, and several of their identifications (the Diet of Worms, events of 1545) assume that year. The dates above supersede them.

## What is still open

- **110 words are not read.** `check_readme.py -v` lists them. Some carry a marked doubt in the line-by-line text; the rest decode to letters that make no word I will stand behind: for example A78r 4 (*oiropi…ssonte*), A78v 27 (*couditouana*, what the strangers at Mirandola are thought to be aiming at), the two places the strangers came from (A79r 1), B174v 13 (*mfcolqe ma fidi…la*, what the Emperor wants instead of Farnese's mediation), B174v 21 (*intaqteahrna*), and most of B175v 6–7.
- **A name code that may be the Emperor's.** At B175r 7 (*con lo … ua doppiamente*) and B175r 24 (*s'el … haueua denari haueria seguitata*) the sense wants the Emperor. The page transcriptions read the code there as L10 (Papa), one of them with a doubt. On bare crops both blind readers found its raised figures unlike the closed ring of the clear L10s (they offered L11, L10 with an open ring, or L19), and one judged the two places to be the same group. The key's L9 for the Emperor is only probable, from one sibling where the raised figure was itself doubtful, though L9 and L10 were told apart blind where they stand side by side at B174r 17. Both places are left as a doubt between L11, which has no value, and L10. A third place, A78v 8 (*fidele uassallo al*), is read L10 and also seems to want the Emperor; the crop cut for it missed the sign, so it has not been tested.
- **Name codes without a value.** L30 (four times in letter A, perhaps once more at A79r 14): the commander on the imperial side to whom Casale would turn for help, who has kept only two thousand foot. L22: a territory he must defend, in which a marquis can sequester church revenues; the State of Milan fits both. L6: named with the Lutherans as the Emperor's ally against the faithful; the Emperor's answer at B174v 10 pairs *dieta et Inghilterra*, so the King of England is likely. L54: a *monsignor* who with Ferrante did Salviati a service with the Emperor. None of these is put into the text.
- **Three groups read from context.** `63` twice where *però* fits; `15` twice inside *commissione* where *mm* is wanted; and a sign pair that decodes *mo* three times in B175r 24 – 175v 4 where *guerra* fits all three sentences. Only `21` = *uu* (*hauuta*, B174r 7) has gone into the key, marked as resting on this letter.
- **The six *ge* places.** *genti* four times and *diligentia* twice are written with a crossed tall stem joined to an r. The three page transcriptions read that group as the single sign `h`, and so did both blind readers on bare crops. All six places make a word only as `t r`, and two editors working apart read it so. Because the shape does not show it to a blind reader, the six are marked as emendations: *[g]enti*, *dili[g]entia*.
- **Letter B is incomplete.** It has no opening formula, date or signature, and the last line breaks off.
- **Persons.** The master of the horse, l'Abbadino, the count of Landriano, messer Camillo, messer Dominichino and the informant in Rome are not identified. The count of Mirandola, Pietro Strozzi, the cardinal of Ferrara, Farnese, Pier Luigi and Salviati are named in the text; who they were is not argued here.

## How it was read

The Estense file holds four sibling letters of June and July 1544 from Ercole to Ferrante whose cipher enclosures were deciphered at the time, with the plain text written out in another hand. Aligning three of them (cc. 96, 103 and 108–109) with their decipherments gave the system: a reciprocal swap of the two halves of a 22-letter alphabet (a↔n, b↔o, c↔p, d↔q, e↔r, f↔s, g↔t, h↔u, i↔x, l↔y, m↔z), written in ordinary italic letters, with figure groups for doubled letters (`14` ll, `18` rr, `19` ss, `2o` tt), nulls (`24`, `25`, `27`), common words (`3o` che, `53` non, `64` perche) and names (`L9` Imperatore, `L10` Papa). The key is [`key.tsv`](key.tsv), with the source of every value; the alignments are `c96_alignment.tsv`, `sib102_codes.tsv` and `sib106_codes.tsv`.

The key was never the difficulty. The scribe's letter forms are: d and the looped sign for l, c and e, a and n, and the long-stemmed h against f followed by a vowel cannot be told apart reliably by shape. So the work was done in two separate steps.

1. **Transcription by shape, frozen before decoding.** Three readers transcribed each page sign by sign from line strips without decoding. The first two worked independently and agreed on 93–95% of paired signs; the third settled their differences by shape. The third reading is frozen with its SHA-256 in [`transcription/FROZEN.sha256`](transcription/FROZEN.sha256), and [`decode/`](decode/) is that reading put through the key by [`decode.py`](decode.py).
2. **Editing against the image, with every change counted.** An editor who reads the Italian settled each page ([`EDIT.md`](EDIT.md)). A sign may be changed to one of its listed look-alikes when the word needs it and the shape allows it. A sign the shape does not allow is marked and shows in brackets. A word that will not come out is left as the frozen reading has it. [`check_edition.py`](check_edition.py) verifies that the Italian is exactly the decode of the edited cipher line and counts every departure by class.

A model-based resolver (the look-alike classes plus an Italian character model, [`resolve.py`](resolve.py)) was tried in place of the editor and is kept as a negative: on a 1544 sibling whose text is known it wrongly changed about 5% of the signs.

## Checks made before publication

The readings, key files and scripts behind this section are in [`blind/`](blind/).

- **Independent second edit of B174r.** A second editor worked from a checkout that had no edition in it. The two texts agreed on 317 of 327 words (96.9%). Two of its readings were better and were adopted (*cose*, B174r 14; *appresenta*, B174r 15); the other differences are a word both leave unread, word division, and doubts that one editor settled and the other kept. The second editor also read *dili[g]entia* in both places, marking the g as supplied.
- **Blind reading of names and numbers.** Two readers were given unlabelled crops and a description of the sign shapes, with no text and no access to the repository, and transcribed by shape. Thirty-one crops covered the place names, personal names and numbers spelled out in the cipher. On 23 both readers gave the edition's signs, exactly or within the listed look-alike pairs; on 6 one reader did; on 2 neither did. Those two are *sei* in *sei milla* (A78v 18), where the middle sign is under a blot and is now marked as supplied, and *Uinesia* (A78v 20), where the crop was faint and cut one sign short. *Ferrara* (A78v 10) has `19` on the page where `18` is needed, as the edition already marks. At the page break *con|tra li* (B174v 1) both readers gave `c` where the edition reads its look-alike `e`.
- **Blind reading of name codes.** Six crops held name codes. L9 and L10 were told apart where they stand side by side. The code at B175r 7 and 24 came back as unlike a clear L10; see the open points. Two crops missed their sign and tested nothing. The codes for Franza, the king and the word codes were not tested.
- **Blind reading of the *ge* group.** The six disputed places were cropped with fifteen controls. Both readers read the disputed group as `h` in all six. Both read four of the five plain `h` controls as `h` (the fifth crop was blank) and four of the five plain `t` controls as `t`, and both read `t r` in *diligentemente* (A78r 10). So a blind reader does not see `t r` in the six places, and they are marked as emendations.
- **Blind reading of the docket.** Described under Dates.
- **Out-of-class changes on B174r.** The page has 15 changes outside the look-alike classes that are not marked as emendations. Thirteen are a sign that one of the first two readers already had; one is a regrouping of two figures into the code `61`; one (*rispetto*, line 23) has no reader behind it and was made independently by the second editor as well.
- **Not done.** The repository's rule is to search the printed editions before working on a cipher. That search has not been made for these letters.

The transcriptions, both edits and the blind readings were made by AI agents (Claude). No palaeographer has checked them.
