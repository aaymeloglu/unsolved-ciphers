# B175r edition notes

The page runs on from B174v ("... et mandar un essercito in Italia oue") and into B175v (L26 "al-" + B175v L01 "meno"). Every departure was checked on the prepared page (rotate -0.25) at 3-6x. Crops are in the scratch directory, not the repo.

## 1. Places not read, or read only in part

- **L5 "a far genti alla Mirandola".** C has `h a g x` ("unti"); the group is the one written in six places in these letters (A78r L9, L10; A78v L26; B174r L2, L6; B175r L5), a crossed tall stem joined to an r. The three page passes read it as `h`, and so did two blind readers given the six places as bare crops. It is read `t r` (plain "ge") because the same form eight signs earlier in A78r L10 was read `t r` by the third reader ("diligentemente"), and because all six places make a word only that way (genti four times, diligentia twice). The reading rests on that, not on the shape alone.
- **L7 "il sore so a o uolti al re s".** This is probably "... et i suoi [pensieri?] sono volti al Re", but I cannot make the letters give it. The signs are left as C has them. The lone `f` (plain s) before "et che" is a long stroke against the following L and may not be a letter at all.
- **L9 "con esso {q/l}e al qual il Papa crede persuader".**
  - The sign after "esso" is left `{d/y}`.
  - "al qual" takes y->d (d/y class) in the second bowl-plus-ascender at x~1515.
  - The construction is still awkward: "con esso, el quale"? "alla quale"?
- **L20 "scritta al praterllo".** The sense wants "al fratello" (Pier Luigi's wife writing to her brother). The page has `c e n g r e 14 b`, a clear cipher c (plain p) plus an extra e (plain r). I left it unemended.
- **L22 "che se di pra a rota della sua ita in Inghilterra".** "rota" may be "rotta" and "sua ita" may be "sua gita/andata". The war of 1545 included the French fleet's descent on England in July 1545. The words are not established, and the signs are left as C has them.
- **L24 "della mo" and L25 "seguitata l n mo".** See section 3: possibly an unvalued group 96.
- **L26 "et uatio de denari al-[meno]".** "uatio" is not read. B175v L01 has "de uati lo" too.

## 2. Emendations (`!`)

- **L1 `a!` (massimame[n]te).** The line ends with a clear St Andrew's x (plain i), not a. This is a scribe's slip at the line end.
- **L3 `d!` ([q]ueste lettre).** The first sign has a long descender: cipher q (plain d), so the page reads "dueste". "queste lettre dice che sono state gratissime al Re" needs cipher d (plain q). This is a slip between the mirrored d/q.
- **L7 `b!` (inclinati[o]ne).** After the tangled g+x at the line start, the sign is a clear looped e (plain r), so the page reads "atirne". Pass A had `{e/o}`. The g+x tangle looks like a correction, so the o may have been lost in it.

## Departures worth naming

The checker counts the rest by class. The class "other" covers three departures.

- **L3 a->y ("lettre").** The a's upright rises well above x-height. glyphs.md: "if the upright rises above x-height, the sign moves toward d or y".
- **L4 t->r (the second "presto").** Pass A has r and pass B has `{t/r}`. The upright is taller than the first presto's r.
- **L26 t->h ("aiutarlo").** Pass A has h, and at 1x I see an h ascender over the body.

## 3. Unvalued code groups

- **L7 `{L10/L11}`.** I settled it as L10 (Papa): "et che con lo Papa va doppiamente". The open second figure matches the L10 at L24.
- **Possible group 96 in L24 and L25.** I did not put it in the text.
  - Both places have `z b` ("mo"), and in both the b is 6-shaped and the z 9-shaped: L24 "per dar conto della [mo] et dira questo Re", L25 "haueria seguitata la [mo]".
  - The feminine "seguitata" and the sense of both sentences fit "impresa": "dar conto della impresa", "haueria seguitata l'impresa".
  - If 96 is a word code, L24 reads "della [96]" and L25 "seguitata la [96]", with the L25 `a` needing an a->n look-alike. Nothing on the other pages tests it.
- **59 (quanto), 61 (quale) and 68 (come)** are key values ("probable" or "sure"). Here 59 occurs twice (L4, L18) in correlatives that make the meaning certain: "presto quanto sia", "cosi strette pratiche col Re quanto forsi con lo Imperatore".

## 4. Persons, places, events

- **"Il Re"** is Francis I.
- **"Lo Imperatore"** is Charles V.
- **"Il Papa"** is Paul III.
- **Farnese** is presumably Pier Luigi Farnese, the Pope's son, invested with Parma and Piacenza in 1545. The marriage alliance ("parentado") is with the French king.
- **"La moglie di Pierluigi"** is Gerolama Orsini.
- **Places:** Perpignan ("Parpignano"), Flanders, Mirandola (a French-held fortress under Galeotto Pico), Milan, Tuscany, the Regno (Naples) and England.
- **"L'armata turchesca"** is the Ottoman fleet allied with France.
- **"V. Ex."** is Ferrante Gonzaga, governor of Milan.

## 5. Choices between two real words

- **L4 "presto presto".** I followed pass A. The repetition may be the encipherer's dittography.
- **L8 "e ito" vs "e imo".** I chose "ito".
- **L14 "haurlo".** C and B have no e. Pass A has "hauerlo".
- **L15 "pigliar a in ogni guisa".** It could also be "pigliara in", the future.
- **L23 "e si auisato".** It could also be "e sia uisato".
