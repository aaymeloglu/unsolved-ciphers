# B175r edition notes

The page runs on from B174v ("... et mandar un essercito in Italia oue") and into B175v (L26 "al-" + B175v L01 "meno"). Every departure was checked on the prepared page (rotate -0.25) at 3-6x. Crops are in the scratch directory, not the repo.

## 1. Places not read, or read only in part

- **L5 "a far genti alla Mirandola".** C has `h a g x` ("unti"); the group is the one written in six places in these letters (A78r L9, L10; A78v L26; B174r L2, L6; B175r L5), a crossed tall stem joined to an r. The three page passes read it as `h`, and so did two blind readers given the six places as bare crops. All six places make a word only as `t r`, plain "ge" (genti four times, diligentia twice), and two editors working apart read it so. Since the shape does not show it to a blind reader, the t is marked as an emendation in all six: `t! r`, shown as [g].
- **L1.** The initial six signs give “e certo” on choosing the e offered by A and C. The c/e form touches the next sign. F. 7r of Barb. lat. 5792 expresses the same thought as “dove si rende certo”.
- **L7.** “Il core sono uolti al re”. The initial sign of “core” has a loop around a long descender, the shape of p; three signs later the long f of “sono” has a straight crossbar. The low sign between its two o vowels is the common n/a confusable. The register has “l’animo, l’inclinatione del Papa et il cuor suo è totalmente volto al Re” (f. 7r). There is one unexplained stroke between re and the conjunction.
- **L9.** “Con esso re, al qual”. The first sign of re has a c-shaped bowl with a raised exit, matching pass B’s e. The register explicitly names the king (f. 7r).
- **L20.** “Scritta al [f]raterllo” preserves the extra r: the letter to be emended is a plain c (plain p), not the round s (plain f). The register has “scritta a suo fratello” on f. 7v. At the end of the line the 6-shaped sign and a free-standing heavy minim make 61, “quale”. Compare 61 on L2 of the same page. This relative clause is absent in the register.
- **L22.** “Che se dira a Roma”. Between x and n of “dira” there is one c-shaped sign with a closed eye (e). The third sign in Roma has a thin 9-tail curling left without a lower g-loop (z). Both readings were obtained from the cipher before comparison with f. 7v, which has “ciò che si dirà a Roma dell’andata sua in Inghilterra”.
- **L24–26 and B175v 1.** The curved 9 and 6-shaped ascender form 96 for *guerra*. This gives *della guerra* and *seguitata la guerra*, followed by a request for money and troops. The same two signs occur before *in Italia* on the verso. F. 7v mentions the war twice, describes continuing it, and specifies *denari et di gente, overo almeno di denari*. On both sides of the page turn the letter group usually read as h occurs in the word for troops. The edition supplies [g] in both places. Pass A reads *lui* on the verso and the edition retains *loi*.

## 2. Emendations (`!`)

- **L1 `a!` (massimame[n]te).** The line ends with a clear St Andrew's x (plain i), not a. This is a scribe's slip at the line end.
- **L3 `d!` ([q]ueste lettre).** The first sign has a long descender: cipher q (plain d), so the page reads "dueste". "queste lettre dice che sono state gratissime al Re" needs cipher d (plain q). This is a slip between the mirrored d/q.
- **L7 `b!` (inclinati[o]ne).** After the tangled g+x at the line start, the sign is a clear looped e (plain r), so the page reads "atirne". Pass A had `{e/o}`. The g+x tangle looks like a correction, so the o may have been lost in it.

## Departures worth naming

The checker counts the rest by class. Several examples follow.

- **L3 a->y ("lettre").** The a's upright rises well above x-height. glyphs.md: "if the upright rises above x-height, the sign moves toward d or y".
- **L4 t->r (the second "presto").** Pass A has r and pass B has `{t/r}`. The upright is taller than the first presto's r.
- **L26 t->h ("aiutarlo").** Pass A has h, and at 1x I see an h ascender over the body.

## 3. Codes

- At lines 7 and 24, the register explicitly names the Emperor (ff. 7r and 7v). The Modena superscript at each place is poorly formed. Readers offered L11, L10 and L19 on crops; the key elsewhere gives L9 for the Emperor and L10 for the Pope. The edition retains the glyph alternatives. Neither occurrence establishes a general value for L11.
- 96 is discussed at lines 24–26 above. Its 9-shaped first sign and the 6-shaped following sign can also look like the letters z and b.

- **59 (quanto), 61 (quale) and 68 (come)** are key values ("probable" or "sure"). Here 59 occurs twice (L4, L18) in correlatives that make the meaning certain: "presto quanto sia", "cosi strette pratiche col Re quanto forsi con lo Imperatore".

## 4. Persons, places, events

- **"Il Re"** is Francis I.
- **"Lo Imperatore"** is Charles V.
- **"Il Papa"** is Paul III.
- **Farnese** is named as the emissary; the text distinguishes him from Pier Luigi’s wife, who wrote the letter mentioned on lines 19–20.
- **"La moglie di Pierluigi"** is Gerolama Orsini.
- **Places:** Perpignan ("Parpignano"), Flanders, Mirandola (a French-held fortress under Galeotto Pico), Milan, Tuscany, the Regno (Naples) and England.
- **"L'armata turchesca"** is the Ottoman fleet allied with France.
- **"V. Ex."** is Ferrante Gonzaga.

## 5. Choices between two real words

- **L4 "presto presto".** I followed pass A. The repetition may be the encipherer's dittography.
- **L8 "e ito" vs "e imo".** I chose "ito".
- **L14 "haurlo".** C and B have no e. Pass A has "hauerlo".
- **L15 “lo pigliara in ogni guisa”.** The signs give the future with the preceding object pronoun. The register has “lo piglierà in ogni guisa” (f. 7r).
- **L23 "e si auisato".** It could also be "e sia uisato".
