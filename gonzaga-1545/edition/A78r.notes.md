# A78r edition notes

Started from `transcription/A78r.C.txt`. Every departure was looked at on the prepared page
(the halves strips, plus 2.5-3.5x crops where needed). `check_edition.py` result: 0 mismatches.
See the current totals in the reading table. The vowel of Pi[e]monte is supplied.

## 1. Unread or open words

- **L03 `con li quali`**. The pointed, nearly filled arch after the b allows a, the alternative already given by reader A. The letter is present.
- **L04 `o il Pi[e]monte`**. The marks between x and c are joined into a loop with a foot, like the sign before x in A79r 1 (*Liuorno*). The tall stroke through the loop comes down from the preceding line. Read it as one y. After `c x`, a darker alteration over the faded ink obscures the vowel. The 9-like sign before the final `b a g r` has an open tail. Reader B took it as z (*m*). The same correspondent spells *Piemonte* in the deciphered sibling, sib106 A18, with `c x r z b a g r`. The same-day parallel in Barb. lat. 5792, f. 49v, names Piedmont and the State of Milan together. These support the place; the vowel itself is supplied. A separate crop reading gives *Piamonte*.
- **L09 `genti imperiali`, L10 `che genti sariano state quelle`**: C has `h n g x` and `h a g x`;
  the group is the one written in six places in these letters (A78r L9, L10; A78v L26; B174r L2, L6; B175r L5), a crossed tall stem joined to an r. The three page passes read it as `h`, and so did two blind readers given the six places as bare crops. All six places make a word only as `t r`, plain "ge" (genti four times, diligentia twice), and two editors working apart read it so. Since the shape does not show it to a blind reader, the t is marked as an emendation in all six: `t! r`, shown as [g].
- **L12 `si trouasse`**. The small cross before `g e b h n 19 r` is a short f crossed by x. Both strokes have their own crossing. Compare the ligature in *si* at A79r 11; and Barb. lat. 5792, f. 49v, on the marquis's “le poche forze ch'egli si trovava”.
- **L16 `semo obgligati`**. The first stem descends below the baseline and crosses an r. The 9-like sign following it has an open, left-turning tail (z, also an alternative in pass A), giving the period verb *semo*. The two short crossed t signs in the next word stand on either side of y x. Both give g, hence the anomalous but legible spelling *obgligati*. At the opening of 17 the sign before S. M.ta is blotted.
- **L17 `Dio gratia`**. Read the first b with the preceding `q x` as *Dio*. Then a short t with its crossbar joined to an accented e gives the start of *gratia*. The page editor recorded precisely this alternative before recognizing the word division.
- **L19 `la [quale] puo star`**. The tall 6-shape and following standalone minim are group 61. Pass A suggested this very regrouping by shape; 61 is *quale* in sib102. The relative resumes V. Ex. after the parenthesis about copies sent to messer Camillo.
- **L21 `uo per se possibil fusse`**: as written. The image has "f r" before "possibil", so it is
  "se". "uo per" (`h b c r e`) may be a slip for "uorrei". On the image the c may be an e, but
  "uorer" is no better, so the c is kept.
- **L27 `[che] nhsuuer?{e/l}i`**: probably "che n'hauerei". `f h` and the blot do not fit, so left
  as C.
- **L24 `persicolo`**: "pericolo" with a stray f (all three readers have a sign there). Left as written.

## 2. Emendations

- **L03 `e!` (`[r]e riseruati`)**: L02 ends "haue" and L03 begins `c r e x ...`. "hauere riseruati
  piu di duamilla fanti" needs e. At 3.5x the first sign is a plain c with a curled top and no eye.
- **L25 `s!` (`amico di [f]ortuna`)**: the page has c (plain p), giving "portuna". "parer poco
  seruitor di S. M.ta et amico di fortuna" needs s (plain f).

Departures marked "other" by the script:

- L18: n > h ("udera", sic for "uedera"). B reads h, and the image shows a tall ascender.
- L29: u > 11 ("difficulta"). Two separate minims at 2.5x, as C's own note allowed.
- L09: 19 > 18 ("la terra"). The figure has a closed g-like lower loop, the 18 form of glyphs.md.

## 3. Code groups

- **`<28>`** (L01, inside "pensau<28>a" = pensaua): it sits inside a word whose letters are complete
  without it, so it is almost certainly a null, like 24, 25 and 27.
- **L3o**. The parallel letter of 4 May in Barb. lat. 5792, ff. 49v–50r, names the marquis in the passages on the defence of Casale, his initial lack of troops and their subsequent recourse to him. The letter to him at ff. 48r–v is headed *Marchese del Vasto*.
- **L22**. The same account names the two territories the marquis had to defend as Piedmont and the State of Milan (f. 49v). The cipher spells Piedmont after L22.
- **`67 58`** (L22): C wrote `6758` as one group. I split it as 67 "pur" + 58 "quando" ("pur quando
  s'hauesse a perder quello di Monferrato"). Both values are in key.tsv, and the sense fits. This
  is a regrouping only, with no change of sign.

## 4. Persons, places, events

- **Casale**: capital of Monferrato.
- **The Franzesi**: threatening to advance and "seguir la uittoria", which suggests a recent French
  success in Piedmont (Ceresole, April 1544).
- **il mastro di stalla**: the Gonzaga master of the horse, sent to Casale.
- **l'Abbadino**: the scribe wrote "abbadino". A Gonzaga agent who has reported to Ferrante.
- **il conte da Ladriano**: sic, for Landriano. The encipherer dropped the n.
- **Madama nostra la Marchesa**: Margherita Paleologa, marchesa of Monferrato, mother of the young
  duke.
- **messer Camillo**: another letter of 4 May calls him the Gonzaga ambassador (Barb. lat. 5792, f. 51v). His surname is open.
- **il duca**: Francesco III Gonzaga, a minor under the cardinal's regency. In L28-29, "quando il
  duca sara in eta" is when he comes of age. The 27 there is a null, and the f n before it was
  read from A.

## 5. Choices between two real words

- **L13**: "labbadino" against "labbadiao". A read `a b`, and the image shows a closed bowl, so
  "l'Abbadino".
- **L05**: "pericolo se non pigliauamo". The page has `f n s 3`. I read the s as 5, joined to the 3
  as 53 "non", and the n as r ("se"). The alternative, "pericolosa f...", makes no sense.

## Summary

The French were expected to advance after their victory. The master of horse was sent to Casale
and fanti were held back to defend it. Casale was ordered to give the French victuals and passage
but to admit no imperial troops without the regent's consent, since help from L3o could not be
counted on. The cardinal now protests that he will do whatever H.M. requires. He would lay down
his life rather than fail his duty, and would rather not lose any of the family's states, even
Monferrato. He fears both looking like a poor servant of the Emperor who goes with fortune, and
the blame for losing a state while he governs for the minor duke.
