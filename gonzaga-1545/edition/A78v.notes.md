# A78v edition notes

Started from `transcription/A78v.C.txt`. Every emendation and every sign added was cut from the
halves strips and enlarged 3-7x. `check_edition.py` result: 0 mismatches. See the current totals in the reading table.
9 emendation sites, among them `t!` in L26 ([g]enti) and `r!` in L18 (s[e]i milla: the sign after the long s is under a heavy blot, and two blind readers gave it as unreadable; "sei" is the only number that fits "s?i milla").

The page runs straight on from A78r ("non ho altro meglior mezzo | che ricorrer a dio") and into
A79r ("ui sono uenuti | doi sconosciuti").

## 1. Unread or open words

- **L02 `uan [io] fussi libero`**: "uan" (`h n a`) is not read. The sense is plainly "if only I were
  as free as many other servants of H.M.", but no look-alike swap turns `h n a` into "se" or "s'".
  Pass A has `{f/h} n` with no a. Left as C.
- **L03 `di mio nipote a carico mio`**. The pointed arch before x allows a as well as n (pass A gives both). Divide *nipote a* here, with *carico* split across the line end. Ercole uses the same terms in his letter of 1 May to the marchese del Vasto (Barb. lat. 5792, f. 48v) about his nephew's state being in his own charge.
- **L08 `al [Imperatore]`**. Immediately after *al*, the L carries one raised figure with an open, left-sweeping tail, read as 9. The blind first-round crop for this occurrence missed the sign. The reading fits Ercole's description of his house as vassals of the Emperor on 4 May (Barb. lat. 5792, f. 51r).
- **L10 `mirand?la`**: the `?` is an ink blot over the sign. "Mirandola" needs b (plain o) there, but
  nothing of the shape shows, so it stays open.
- **L16 `dal re ?? dopo della uittoria`**: two signs under a heavy blot, perhaps a cancellation.
  "dal re [di Francia]" or similar may be meant. Not read.
- **L21 `mandar [che] a fra doi do un homo`**: the sense is that the ambassador would send a man to
  Mirandola within two days, but "[che] a" and "do" do not parse. Left as written.
- **L24 `per [quanto] egli dice hauer compreso`**. The third letter has a small hooked head (reader A gave c/e); then the short s-shaped 5 and open-tailed 9 together form group 59, *quanto*, also used at A79r 7 in *di quanto intendero*. Read r with the following t y x as *egli*.
- **L26 `uinno`**: sic for "uanno" ("che uanno et uengono incognite"). The x is clear.
  **`oensino`** for "pensino" (b for c) are also left as written.
- **L27 `cose di Toscana`**. The first long stem crosses an r with an upturned end. The second crosses the head of a p with a distinct straight descender, different from the adjacent g's loop and the terminal h in *piu*. Pass A noted a second stroke descending below the line, and B gave p as an alternative. The cipher reads `p b f r | q x | g b f p n a n`. The strangers came from Pisa and Livorno on the following page.


## 2. Emendations

- **L01 `t!` (`pre[g]allo`)**: "ricorrer a dio et pregallo che mi aiuti". At 4x the sign is a clear h
  (stem and arched leg, no descender); the sense needs t (plain g). The same h/t slip was emended on
  A78r L09.
- **L07 `e!` (`fa[r]ia`)**: "non sara tale che faria che manchi mai al debito". At 5x it is a plain r
  with a long flat top and no eye or loop; the sense needs e (plain r).
- **L09 `a! n!` (`cardi[n][a]l`)**: "la passata del cardinal di ferrara". At 7x there are three minims
  (an m, plain z) between the x and the looped y; "cardinal" needs `a n`, a bowl plus two minims.
  Pass A reads `a n`, but the cut shows no bowl. The same word in L11 and L23 is written with a
  plain `a n`.
- **L10 `18!` (`fe[rr]ara`)**: all three passes read 19 (ss), and at 5x the figure is a 9 with an
  open, straight tail, not the 8 of L23, where "ferrara" has a clear 18. Ferrara needs rr.
- **L11 `b!` (`si p[o] ritrar`)**: the sign after the c is a closed, ink-filled bowl with a tail to
  the right, an a. It has no ascender (b) and is not round (o). "si po ritrar", "can withdraw".
- **L14 `b!` (`allh[o]ra`)**: a plain open c at 4x, with no ascender. "et che allhora largamente la
  ragguagliaria".
- **L18 `b!` (`pers[o]ne`)**: "da sei milla persone". At 4x the sign after the f is the same plain c
  as in L14.
- **L20 `n!` (`ambasci[a]tor`)**: "una lettra del ambasciator del re in uinesia". There is a single
  minim (with a small head) between the x and the g. "ambasciator" needs n (plain a), two minims.
  The 1 is not followed by a figure, so it is not a doubling group either.

## 3. Code groups

- **15** (L16, *commissione*). C wrote `1 {s/5}`; the 1 and the 5 stand together
  and are joined here into one group with no change of sign. It stands where "mm" is needed. The
  same group stands in the same word on B175v L09 (*commirssione*). So 15 is very probably the
  doubling sign for mm. In L19 the same word is written `p b 16 x 19 x b a r` ("connissione"), with
  the nn group 16 where mm is meant.



## 4. Persons, places, events

- **Imperatore**: Charles V. **V. Ex.**: Ferrante Gonzaga, the addressee.
- **la Mirandola, il conte della Mirandola, il conte**: Galeotto II Pico, count of Mirandola, a French
  partisan.
- **il cardinal di Ferrara**: Ippolito II d'Este, the French party's cardinal.
- **Bondeno**: Ferrarese town on the Panaro, near Mirandola, where the count went to meet the cardinal.
- **la Signoria**: Venice. **uinesia** is the scribe's spelling of Venetia.
- **il re**: Francis I. **l'ambasciator del re in uinesia**: the French ambassador at Venice.
- **Pietro Strozzi** (`strozi`): the Florentine exile in French service. Strozzi and the count
  writing to the king that they had six thousand men together, waiting only for an order, matches
  Strozzi's levy at Mirandola in the spring of 1544. **la uittoria** is then Ceresole (April 1544),
  as on A78r. That is an inference from the text; the date of the letter is not checked here.
- **messer Dominichino**: an informant who assures the cardinal of the news. Not identified.

## 5. Choices between two real words

- **L26 "genti"**: C has `h a g x`; the group is the one written in six places in these letters (A78r L9, L10; A78v L26; B174r L2, L6; B175r L5), a crossed tall stem joined to an r. The three page passes read it as `h`, and so did two blind readers given the six places as bare crops. All six places make a word only as `t r`, plain "ge" (genti four times, diligentia twice), and two editors working apart read it so. Since the shape does not show it to a blind reader, the t is marked as an emendation in all six: `t! r`, shown as [g]. The feminine "incognite" agrees.
- **L02 "fussi" against "cussi"**: s (plain f) and 5 (cc) have the same shape. "fussi libero" decides
  it, so C's doubt `{s/5}` is settled as s.
- **L23 "col cardinal"**: C has `p {i/?} {u/n} e`. On the image there is a dotted minim and then two
  minims between the p and the e. Pass A has a single sign there. I read one n (plain a), which gives
  "cardinal" as in L11. Read as two signs, it would give no word.
- **L20 "una lettra"**: C has nothing between the h and the y. On the image there is an inked,
  partly overwritten cluster after the h. Pass B reads `b a {n/m}` there; I read `a n`, giving "una".

Other departures outside the look-alike classes, all supported by a pass and the image: L12 r > t
("gli", B `{t/r}`), L13 l > t ("signoria", A `{t/l}`), L14 k > r ("largamente", A `{r/t}`), L23 l > y
("col"). The three `f!` insertions of the earlier draft in L17 ("strozi", "scritto") and L18 ("insieme") turned out to be
real long-s signs in light ink that pass B also has. They are now ordinary added signs, not
emendations.

## Summary

The cardinal will turn to God. He wishes he were as free as other servants of the Emperor, and
promises to show his loyalty openly, though more reservedly because of the state he governs. Then
the news: after Cardinal d'Este passed through, the count of Mirandola met him at Bondeno. The
cardinal would commit himself to nothing until he had dealt with Venice. Messer Dominichino reports
that no commission has yet come from the king. The count and Pietro Strozzi have written that they
have six thousand men together and wait only for the order to march. A letter has come from the
French ambassador in Venice saying he will send a man to Mirandola. Fanti are coming and going there
in secret, and their aim is suspected.
