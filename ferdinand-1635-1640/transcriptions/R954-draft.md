# R954: draft reading and revisions

Source: [DECODE R954](https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/954), images I4976/I4977. Manual reading from the downloaded facsimiles, subsequently collated with R1889. This is a working transcription, not a definitive diplomatic edition. Ciphertext was source-assisted from XZ's transcription, so the overall exercise is **not a blind independent retranscription**.

Latin letter forms are represented with ordinary Unicode; u/v and i/j are retained where practicable, not guaranteed at every occurrence. Superscript abbreviations use parentheses; square brackets mark an uncertain or editorial reading; `~~...~~` is cancelled text; `⟨...⟩` is an insertion. Abbreviation expansions and modern word spacing belong to the reading edition, not this witness. Underlining marks passages selected for encipherment; it is described rather than replicated below.

P1 main-column line numbers start with “Lrae”, **excluding the salutation**, whereas R1889 body line numbers include its salutation. The E/F alignment tables use phrase anchors for P2 because its revisions interleave lines.

## P2: main column, top revision

Cancelled/rearranged material includes:

```text
fuit cum Gallo [vexilla regia ...]
etiam cum ... [Gallo?]
⟨eandem occuparet⟩
[...], id superando
vexilla regia [hostium ...]
```

This cancelled block is **not fully transcribed**. The cipher margin instead has `etiam fuit cum 17 n 18 19 y 25 ...`, corresponding to the target's “uti etiam fuit cum Gallus vexilla regia superans eandem occuparet.” The exact development of this revision needs paleographic review. Do not silently substitute the target's recovered words for the partly illegible draft deletion.

D2.06–12 are a **revision zone**, not one continuous grammatical sentence as transcribed. In particular, the ciphertext's “uti convenit tum”, extra *fuit*, and placement of *sustentationem* must not be described as verbatim copying of an unrevised draft. D2.03–04 explicitly name Trier and read *circumuicinatibus*; R1889 instead goes directly from *in dicta civitate* to a form corresponding to *locisque circumiacentibus*. D2.05 reads *sumat*, whereas the literal target gives *sumet*.
