## The two remaining discrepancies

**V01:6, *civitas*.** The draft writes *ciuitas*. Both the draft cipher margin and the target have a 7-like sign where a is required; under the otherwise stable alphabet 7=c. A curve beneath the end of the word is visible, but it does not securely identify a replacement sign. The final edition therefore reads **[civitas]**, while the literal stream remains *ciuitcs*. This may be an inherited encipherment/copying error or a glyph distinction not yet understood. The draft margin is likely ancestral to the copy and is not independent proof that the suspect sign must mean a.

**V03:3, *iam*.** The draft writes *iam*, but the target's b/6-like form has been transcribed 6, which normally gives e. The 1640 letter supplies a real distinction between `b=m` (in *etiam* and *quam*) and `6=e` (in *praeiudicium* and *velimus*). Comparison with the 1635 forms still does not let us assign its disputed mark to b solely by handwriting. The final edition reads **[iam]**, while the literal stream remains *iae*. Assigning this particular sign to b would be a contextual emendation, not additional visual validation.

A cleaner image, a matching original key with glyph exemplars, or an independent fair copy could establish the exact mechanism. Simply substituting a and m in these two positions would produce 95/95 agreement but add no evidence.
