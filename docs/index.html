Unsolved ciphers

Attempts on historical ciphers that are short, context-rich, and listed as unsolved. Started after Vals AI reported Claude Fable 5.1 reading Thomas Urquhart's Cyphral Distich; the question was whether the same approach, an agent plus a person checking its work, gets anywhere on the rest of the list.

1432 The five castles of the Florentine campaignBernardo de’ Medici to Averardo, Lorenzana, 17 April 1432 · ASF MAP V, c. 208r Deciphered Ordered to dismantle five castles, Bernardo questions whether Chianni can be relieved and whether his soldiers are really to plunder the inhabitants. A glossed December letter supplies values for 294 of the 339 cipher signs. Italian, English and cipher signs · Key and verification.
1644 Les voyes de prudenceSir Richard Forster, 13 May 1644 · AD Val-d'Oise, 68 H 8 Deciphered A ciphered passage in a letter by the treasurer of Henrietta Maria's household: counsel that preserving one's life is no failing before God. Mixed letter-and-number homophonic substitution, 207 symbols, three repairs. George Lasry and Norbert Biermann had read it earlier, and Robert Pitt published a key on 14 September 2026. Ciphertext, French and English · With the 37 cipher words and the key. Write-up, solver and verification.
1653 Dutch cipher in ThurloeVande Perre to de Bruyne, London, August–November 1653 · Birch, Thurloe State Papers i Decipheredtwo fragments open Paulus van de Perre’s twenty ciphered runs, left as numbers in the intercepted English translations: a reasonable disposition to negotiate, the government awaiting orders with impatience, Harrison and the Anabaptists losing influence, the new Council of State “reckoned to our advantage”, ten ships run, want of masts. A 22-letter alphabetical substitution recovered from the printed digits; Birch’s English glosses agree, and they supply five code meanings. In the earliest letter: “a character to open a lockt chest, ick segghe een cijffer”, I mean a cipher. Digits, Dutch and English · Write-up, transcription, key, verification.
1513 The Florentine ambassador at AmiensRoberto Acciaiuoli, Amiens, 23 October 1513 · ASF MAP LXXII, doc. 192 Substantially decipheredshort of the 95% bar Louis XII has ordered Monsieur de Bourbon into Burgundy with lances and Landsknechte. News of a rout overturns a plan Acciaiuoli had been discussing; the king is torn between fear of the Swiss and anger at people who yielded to the English, sends word that he will fight if the Swiss move, and the prelates are to leave for Rome after All Saints. Acciaiuoli’s own key survives as Gabbrielli’s no. 120 and confirms the values first taken from Brice Dudout’s Pandolfini key. 92% of the 1,887 signs are accepted into a continuous reading and about 90% of the words are read with nothing supplied; about 50 words rest on a bracketed supply, about 22 are unread, and codes 15, 26 and 47 are unidentified. Italian reading, line by line · Write-up, key, transcriptions, verifier and review.
1544 The cardinal of Mantua to his brotherErcole Gonzaga to Ferrante Gonzaga, 9 January and 4 May 1544 · Biblioteca Estense, Autografoteca Campori, Gonzaga Ercole, sfasc. III, cc. 77–79, 174–175 DecipheredJanuary at the 95% benchmark, May at 93.8% Ercole asks Ferrante whether his informant on the Pope’s dealings with France has been duped. His confidential letter-book dates the dispatch to 9 January and preserves its missing ending. In May he defends his orders for Casale and reports that two strangers at Mirandola came from Pisa and Livorno. The January letter clears 95% on the edited cipher text by four words. The May letter stands at 93.8%. Twenty readings are counted as supplied after a blind check. WAINUT read both letters in part first; this is a fuller reading. Seventeen words remain unread. Italian and English · Write-up, key, transcriptions, edition and checks.
1568 The Regent's postscriptMoray to John Wood, Edinburgh, 13 July 1568 · BL Add MS 32091 f. 213v Substantially deciphered Four ciphered lines at the foot of a letter to the Regent's agent at Elizabeth's court, on the day Mary was moved out of Carlisle: her being allowed to stay there, and the sending home of Lord Fleming, "hes done greit evil"; the warning about her return remains partly unread. The conjectural ending “as our friends know” requires an unexplained sign to be omitted. A simple substitution with homophones and word-signs, read from the page image; two person-signs and the last word open. Cipher, Scots and English · Write-up, transcription, key, verification.
1589 Carta en cifra de VeneciaOttobon to Mocenigo, 27 April 1589 · BNE Mss/994, ff. 34–38 Substantially deciphered A Venetian dispatch to the ambassador in France, with the enclosed reply to Henri III's envoy Gondi, who had come to ask for aid and a league. The Republic's surviving key, Ziffra prima, reads all seven cipher pages. Intercepted in Savoy in 1589 and broken then by Philip II's cipher secretary, whose working was lost. Page-by-page reading · Manuscript images, Italian and English side by side. Write-up, transcription, key excerpt and decoder.
1634 Ferdinand correspondenceStuttgart / Brussels / Vienna, 28 October 1634, 16 November 1635 and 22 February 1640 Substantially deciphered1634 has unresolved passages A French Rhine expedition, winter quarters at Trier, and recruitment in Westphalia with a request for 100,000 florins. The separate 1634 cipher has six unresolved spans and three conjectural readings; its literal output and editorial changes remain explicit. A known Latin draft supplies the 1635 alphabet; a separate syllable supplement extends the reading through both 1640 pages. Uncertain glyphs and copying errors remain explicit. Three letters, their readings and evidence · Readings, key evidence, transcriptions and decoder.
1758 Chiffre aus ParisStarhemberg, Paris, 23 May 1758 · Manuscript collation Substantially deciphered A complete proposed reading of a substantially deciphered letter about bringing Spain into the Seven Years’ War. The 1752 key identifies Farinelli’s statement to the recipient alongside Wall’s statement to the French ambassador. Photographs support 1,726 manuscript digits; eleven conjectural repair sites and several uncertain words remain explicit. Cipher rows, German and English · With graded source output, a separate repair ledger and reproducible checks. Write-up, keys, scripts and audit.
1492 Niccolini at Pavia and VigevanoAngelo Niccolini to Piero de’ Medici, 3 August 1492 to January 1493 · ASF, Mediceo avanti il Principato LXXIV 42, 49, 81, 90 Partialheld-out tests failed Four short cipher passages in the embassy cipher of the 1494 letters, with Niccolini’s own sign table built from his glossed letters. 39 of 46 words have a proposed reading, 8 of them free of any choice between sign values; 4 are unread and 3 doubtful. His glosses give 22 as Lodovico Sforza and 24 as Ascanio, so the envoys’ name list is not the key sheet’s. Two held-out tests set beforehand missed their bars: 0.358 against 0.85 with the sheet key, and 0.527 against 0.80 with his own table. 1494 Florentine orators at the Milanese courtAlamanni, Vigevano, 26 April 1494 · Ridolfi, Milan, 16, 21 and 30 October 1494 · ASF, Mediceo avanti il Principato LXXIV Partial Both men used the embassy cipher whose key sheet survives in the same archive (MAP LXXII 183). In April Alamanni privately urges the Neapolitan stroke against Genoa as the one remedy, done in time and under cover. In October Ridolfi reports the King at Pavia with Duchess Bona, the death of Gian Galeazzo Sforza and Milanese opinion that Florence cannot resist. Ridolfi's three letters have a proposed reading for all but two groups, Alamanni's for 80 of 91 words; more than half of Alamanni's words and two thirds of Ridolfi's are free of a choice between sign values. On a held-out deciphered sibling the key output of a blind transcription agreed with the printed text at 0.838, under the 0.85 bar set beforehand. Alamanni, April 1494 · Ridolfi, October 1494 · Write-up, key, transcriptions, verifier and review.
1524 Letters to the Marquis of MantuaSuardino, Vitoria?, 26 February 1524 · f. 68r, January 1524 · Capino, Robbio, 8 April 1524 · BAV Vat. lat. 8214, ff. 298r-v, 68r, 198r Partial Three leaves sent to Federico II Gonzaga share one homophonic cipher, with no surviving key. In February Suardino reports from the Imperial court on Carpi, granted to Prospero Colonna without investiture. In January a "we" told not to ride out feigns illness, with urine "made to be dyed on purpose with blood", the deception Federico confessed to Castiglione; its covering letter dates it by 29 January. In April Capino writes that the army will fall back "from river to river, and then to Pavia, Cremona and Milan", "as Barbon says". The key was rebuilt from the text alone, and 142 of 227 segments read strictly. On new ciphertext in Vat. lat. 8211, transcribed blind from a poor microfilm, the frozen key read 30% of segments against 11% by chance, under the 40% bar set beforehand. Italian and English, leaf by leaf · Write-up, key, transcriptions, verifier, held-out test and review.
