Unsolved ciphers / write-up and files

Archives départementales du Val-d'Oise, 68 H 8 · France, 13 May 1644

Les voyes de prudence

A ciphered passage in a letter in the hand of Sir Richard Forster, treasurer of Henrietta Maria's household: spiritual counsel to someone in danger, that preserving one's life is no failing before God.

What the passage says

Forster wrote from France in May 1644. The plaintext names no person, place or title, so the addressee is inferred, not established. Karen Britland's abstract says Henrietta Maria, at Exeter, ill, pregnant, and deciding whether to flee to France, made a related request a few days later, which makes her a plausible addressee, not a certain one. Whether Forster composed the counsel or relayed a confessor's is open.

How it was read

The cipher is a mixed letter-and-number homophonic substitution: 34 symbols over 207 tokens, in 37 comma-separated words. Character n-gram hill climbing went nowhere at that length. A beam search over a French lexicon with 17th-century spellings, constrained only by same-symbol-same-letter, produced most of the key in one run; a dozen symbols were then fixed by hand. The 16 letter-symbols map to 16 distinct plaintext letters, and the 18 number-symbols to 16. A Codex agent re-applied the key mechanically and reproduced the reading. Everything is reproducible from the published files.

Earlier readings

Satoshi Tomokiyo's list still carried this passage as undeciphered when we read it on 14 September 2026, and we found no published solution. There were earlier ones. Britland has since told Robert Pitt that George Lasry supplied a decipherment after her 2013 article and that Norbert Biermann reached the same solution independently. Pitt published his own key a few hours before ours on the same day. Pitt's key and ours were reached separately and are identical; the earlier reading Britland passed on differs in a few words.

ie vous en responds et mesmes dans

les reigles de perfection. Prenez s[e]ulement les voyes de prudence p[o]ur concerver votre vie, pour en faire a Dieu un plus grand sacrifi[c]e par la multiplication des vos services pour le salut de vos freres;

Modernised

Il n'y a aucun sujet de scrupule de manquer à Dieu, je vous en réponds, et même dans les règles de perfection. Prenez seulement les voies de prudence pour conserver votre vie, pour en faire à Dieu un plus grand sacrifice par la multiplication de vos services pour le salut de vos frères ; et mesurer à cela s'il est meilleur d'agir ou de subir.

English

There is no cause for scruple about failing God, I answer to you for it, even by the rules of perfection.

Only take the ways of prudence to preserve your life, so as to make of it a greater sacrifice to God through the multiplication of your services for the salvation of your brethren; and measure by that whether it is better to act or to endure.

13 May 1644

Notes

  • Word 13, prenez s[e]ulement: cipher a (d) where 2 (e) is needed. A handwritten 2 and a are easily confused.
  • Word 16, p[o]ur: 16 (i) where s (o) is needed.
  • Word 27, sacrifi[c]e: g (y) where q (c) is needed; g and q are confusable in hand.
  • Word 17, conceruer: the key gives concerver, a spelling attested in period documents, so it is not repaired.
  • The last clear word is soupir in Tomokiyo's transcription and in Britland's printed text, but soubir in her abstract, that is subir, to endure. “To act or to endure” is the better reading.
  • Word breaks inside cipher words are unmarked: descrupule, manquera (manquer a), lesreigles, prenezseulement, lesvoyesde, enfaire, parla, lesalut.
