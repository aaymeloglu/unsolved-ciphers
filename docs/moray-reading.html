Regent Moray to John Wood, 13 July 1568

The cipher postscript of a letter from the Regent of Scotland to his secretary at Elizabeth's court, BL Add MS 32091 f. 213v (DECODE R8345), read from the page image. A simple substitution with a few homophones and word-signs, in Scots, with word spacing.

The allowing of [the Queen] to remain in Carlisle, and the sending home of Lord Fleming, has done great harm. If the [Queen] comes home there, […] no more to ruin the whole […] our friends […].

Mary was moved from Carlisle to Bolton on 13–15 July 1568, the date of the letter; Lord Fleming was back in Scotland in arms by 21 August. The two person-signs are read as the Queen from context only.

A proposed ending, still unproved

Gif the [Q2] cummis haom thair, lakkis na mair to s(p)il the haill es oure freindis knauis.
If [the Queen] comes home, nothing more is needed to ruin the whole, as our friends know.

Visual comparison favours ?nau?is. With contextual k and the old fifth-sign value o, the literal result is knauois, not knauis (knows). The fifth sign differs from ordinary o-signs, but reading knauis requires omitting it without a demonstrated cancellation or null. Reading es (as) also requires an unproved value for the preceding singleton.

The earlier eight-hit argument for lattis is withdrawn: six hits were broken prelattis (prelates). DOST attests both lattis and lakkis, as well as es and knauis; these establish possible words, not this reading.

The full manuscript review records the glyph comparisons and alternatives, with saved OCR evidence and reproducible conditional readings. The archive search found no matching key or additional same-key ciphertext. NRAS217 Box 45 is a specific location lead whose relevance remains unproved.

Baseline key

No matching key or contemporary decipherment has been identified. The John Wood → Cecil line on the decipherer's specimen sheet Add MS 4136 f. 33 uses the same glyph repertoire in a different key.

Open points

  • Carlyle (4 A c a Z5 a c): the two c-shapes have to be r and e for the name and are the same shape; the last is read as a slip for e. Z5 is y (Bain: "Carlyle", "haym") or i ("Carlile", "haim").
  • suffering (Zh P g x o Ab xd Tb): P is the u/v glyph; the single curl g stands for f or ff.
  • haom thair: the glyphs are in the order h-a-o-m; read as hame (home) with a transposed letter, or an unrecognised spelling.
  • la??is: doubled Zz, also opening L4. Both lattis and lakkis are attested in DOST. The former eight-versus-zero corpus preference for t is withdrawn: six of the eight hits were hyphenated fragments of prelattis, and two genuine verb hits cannot exclude k.
  • s(p)il: s occurs once; spil (spoil, ruin) by sense.
  • haill e?: the t-shaped sign at L3.26 is unresolved. Es is an attested spelling of as, making e+s possible; haille followed by a word-sign as is another unproved division.
  • Line 4: seven visible signs. Visual comparison favours ?nau?is; the first sign remains k/t, and the fifth sign's identification as ordinary o is disputed. Contextual k with the old o value gives knauois. Knauis (knows) requires omitting the fifth sign; knawis requires a different unproved deletion and value. No cancellation, null or compound w has been demonstrated. The earlier negative DOST headword search for ??a?ois tested a disputed transcription; it does not eliminate the new alternatives.
  • The person-signs 4̄ and Ē, and the lone dotted o read as and.
