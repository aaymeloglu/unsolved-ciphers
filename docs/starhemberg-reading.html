Paris, 23 May 1758 · 21 manuscript cipher lines · 1,726 digits

Chiffre aus Paris

Starhemberg on the memorandum to Wall, Farinelli’s matching statement, and hopes that ceding Minorca to Spain would bring Spain into the war.

Partial decipherment · Continuous proposed reading

Reading and evidence

The surviving 1752 Austrian Prima/Secunda key supports a connected reading of the letter. The central sentence pairs Farinelli speaking to the recipient with Wall speaking to the French ambassador: their closely agreeing statements raise hopes of Spanish participation. The letter also concerns forwarding reports, the memorandum delivered to Wall, and further confidential negotiations.

This is a reconstruction with eleven conjectural digit-edit sites, four contextual readings of 99 as g, and several uncertain key variants. An exact decipherment is not established. Square brackets flag substantial restorations or lexical doubts. Spelling, word boundaries, inflection and expansion of abbreviated key entries are editorial throughout; unbracketed prose does not certify every ending.

Manuscript photographs supplied by Satoshi Tomokiyo, with Alexandre Pillon’s permission, on 21 September 2026 support the collation. The margin shows five digits absent from the Cryptiana transcription: 5 at the end of V1, 11 at V2, and 31 at V9. The photographed digits are preserved separately from the proposed repairs.

R means recto and V verso. Cipher paragraph boundaries fall within some manuscript lines, so boundary lines repeat below. Spacing and punctuation retain transcription conventions; control placements require explicit interpretation. The final digit of the group adopted as 31405 in V5 may be 5 or 6.

Cleartext opening

Manuscript prose

German

Hochgebohrner Herr Graf!

Ew. Hochgebohrn letztere an den Hof erstattete Bericht-Schreiben durch die Post, und jenes, so Dieselbe durch einen aus Cadix gekommenen französischen Courier abgehen lassen, habe richtig erhalten, und an des Herrn Hof-Canzlers Excellenz gehörig eingeschickt, und zumahlen das letztere noch den nämlichen Tag, als ich es überkommen, durch einen eben zur Abreise fertig gestandenen Courier ohne Verzug weiters expediert.

English

High-born Count,

I have duly received Your Lordship's latest reports to the court sent by post, and the one you sent through a French courier who had come from Cadiz. I forwarded them to His Excellency the Court Chancellor. In particular, I sent the latter onward without delay on the very day I received it, through a courier who was already ready to depart.

Reading note

Lightly normalized manuscript prose. The word gehörig and some endings remain less secure; these are not decoded cipher groups.

The memorandum to Wall

Ciphertext and reconstructed reading

German

So [stattlich?], als ich nun den Innhalt derselben gefunden, so große Hoffnun[g] mache mir, daß [es] deroselben annoch gelingen werde, die durch das dem Stat[s]secretario Wall behändigte sehr treflich gefasste Memoir so geschickt als vor[sich]ti[g] eingeleitete Handlung nunmehro immer offen zu behalten und den spanischen Hof [bis zu] solchen Resolutionen zu vermögen, die entweder dem Krieg eine vergnüglichere, als die dermahlige Gestalt ist, geben oder uns einen erspr[ieß]lichen [†] honorablen Frieden verschaffen könnten;

English

[Impressive?] as I found their contents, I have great hopes that you will still succeed in keeping open the negotiation so skillfully and cautiously initiated through the excellently drafted memorandum delivered to Secretary of State Wall, and in persuading the Spanish court to adopt decisions that would either give the war a more favorable turn than its present state or secure for us an advantageous and honorable peace.

Reading note

The first cipher paragraph ends partway through V1. † marks the defective 119 at the recto/verso join. Deleting it and restoring an encoded en have equal edit cost; no extra substantive word is claimed.

Farinelli, Wall and Minorca

Ciphertext and reconstructed reading

German

und gibt mir zumahlen die von dem Farinelli gegen Euer Hochgebohrn und von dem Wall gegen den französischen Botschafter fast zu gleicher Zeit gemachte, genau gleichförmige Äusserung sehr große Hoffnun[g], daß schon Minorca an Spanien überlassen würde, wozu sich Frankreich [bereit?] zeige un[d] wohl leicht [en]tschließen wird, alsdann dieser Hof an dem Krieg mit Theil nehmen und andurch den [engländischen] und preußischen Hof in sehr große Verlegenheit setz[en] würde.

English

In particular, the closely agreeing statement made by Farinelli to Your Lordship and by Wall to the French ambassador, at nearly the same time, gives me very great hope that Minorca would indeed be ceded to Spain. France shows itself [ready?] to do this and will probably readily resolve upon it. This court would then participate in the war and thereby place the English and Prussian courts in very great difficulty.

Reading note

The paragraph begins within V1 and ends within V8, so these boundary lines appear in adjacent sections. Farinelli and no are both explicit values of Secunda 31183. The longer ge-variants remain tentative. Schon is rendered as indeed; the passage expresses hope, not an unconditional Spanish promise.

The courier and further negotiations

Ciphertext and reconstructed reading

German

Da ich nun Euer Hochgebohrn durch den Courier Zinner zu erstattenden Berichten mit vielem Ver[langen?] entgegensehe, so brauche indessen nichts Be[s]eres einrathen, als in dem bereits eingeschlagenen Weeg fortzufahren und den Wall so viel nur immer möglich durch dero [g]schicktes Benehmen zu weiteren Vertrauenausserungen zu vermögen.

English

As I await with great [eagerness?] the reports Your Lordship is to send through the courier Zinner, I can advise nothing better in the meantime than to continue along the course already taken, and through your skillful dealings persuade Wall, as far as possible, to make further confidential disclosures.

Reading note

Verlangen remains uncertain. The two digits 31 at the end of V9 are visible. Ziner is normalized to Zinner; the code 99 keeps its ordinary Secunda value er here. Other occurrences interpreted as g are explicit inferences.

Cleartext closing

Manuscript prose

German

Ich beharre mit aller Hochachtung

Ew. Hochgebohrn

gehorsamster Diener

Georg Graf und Herr von Starhemberg

Paris den 23ten May 1758.

English

I remain, with the greatest respect, Your Lordship's most obedient servant,

Georg, Count and Lord of Starhemberg

Paris, 23 May 1758.

Reading note

Signature and date are visible. The recipient’s personal name is not supplied by the letter; Rosenberg remains a contextual identification.

What remains uncertain

  • Opening adjective: the roots so / stat? / lich suggest stattlich, but the wording and translation “impressive” remain tentative.
  • Bereit and Verlangen: contextual readings. The key gives berg at the group adopted as 31405; the alternative 31406 does not establish bereit. The letters of 31427 remain uncertain.
  • Gegen: longer handwritten ge-variants at Prima 31115 and Secunda 31010/31126 appear to give gege. Their exact letters remain a paleographic judgment, supported by three contexts.
  • Four 99 values: Hoffnung twice, vorsichtig and gschicktes require an inferred g. The numerical tables give Prima 99 = man and Secunda 99 = er. Alphabetical g headings have gg-like forms resembling 99, which could explain an encipherer’s mistake but do not establish a general 99 = g mapping. Secunda er remains appropriate in Zi / n / er, normalized to Zinner.
  • Framing: unmarked 883 occurs at both boundaries. Treating the pair as framing is a hypothesis not documented by the inspected instructions. Both groups remain in the source.
  • Punctuation and the peace join: the initial 854 boundary requires interpreting a displaced mark. The manuscript’s defective 119 at the recto/verso join is suppressed in the proposed reading; an alternative restoring en has equal edit cost.
  • Minorca: the literal connective is scho / n. Its rendering as “indeed,” punctuation, and the relation of the clauses are editorial. The passage is not evidence of an unconditional Spanish promise.
  • Recipient: the letter supplies no personal name. Rosenberg is a contextual possibility. “This court” is interpreted as the Spanish court, although the author writes from Paris.

Further progress could come from a complete audit of the key’s alternatives and contemporary decipherments using the same key, especially evidence for the encipherer’s treatment of 99 and repeated 55. The current reconstruction does not uniquely prove its repairs.

Reproducibility and evidence grades

The manuscript parse conserves all 1,726 digits, with 413 units and two structurally invalid groups. The separately emended stream has 1,721 digits, 404 units and no invalid groups. Its length matching the Cryptiana baseline is coincidental. Table switches remain Prima 876 → Secunda and Secunda 899 → Prima.

Source grades: 398 H, 12 M, 3 I. Emended grades: 378 H, 8 M, 18 I. H denotes historical key evidence; M uncertainty; I inference. Counts include nulls and controls. The three source I units are the framing pair and displaced punctuation. These grades do not measure authenticated prose or transcription accuracy.
