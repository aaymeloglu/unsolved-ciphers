Starhemberg, Paris, 23 May 1758 · Cryptiana transcription, 22 rows, 1,721 digits · Read with the 1752 Austrian Prima/Secunda key

Chiffre aus Paris

A ciphered letter from Prince Starhemberg, Austria's ambassador at Versailles, to a colleague at the Spanish court, in the spring of 1758: on the memorandum handed to Ricardo Wall, on Minorca as the price of Spain's entry into the war, and on the courier who is to carry the next reports. Read with the two-table numerical key the Vienna chancellery issued in 1752.

Partial decipherment · system identified · exact full text open

What the letter says

Two years into the Seven Years' War, Austria and France wanted Spain in. Starhemberg writes that the memorandum handed to Wall, the Spanish first secretary, was very well drafted and that the negotiation it opened must be kept alive until the Spanish court comes to a decision. That could either give the war a better shape than it now has or bring an honourable peace. Closely matching statements by the recipient and the French ambassador raise hopes that Minorca, which France had taken from Britain in 1756, would be ceded to Spain, and that Spain would then enter the war and embarrass the English and Prussian courts. He is waiting for reports by the courier Zinner, and meanwhile advises staying on the course already taken and coaxing further confidences out of Wall.

The recipient's name is not in the recovered text. The Vienna archive catalogues five 1758 letters from Starhemberg to Franz von Rosenberg, the Austrian envoy in Madrid, on exactly these subjects, so Rosenberg is the likely addressee, but the catalogue does not show that 23 May is among them.

How it was read

The cipher is numerical. Ordinary units are two digits; a leading 3 opens a four-digit code, so most written units are five digits. A standalone 8 is a null. Marked three-digit units supply punctuation, dates and table changes: here 876 switches from the Prima table to the Secunda at digit 662, 899 switches back at 1349, and Secunda 897 is a period. The tables are the 1752 key in DECODE R1695 and R1698, with the chancellery's own instructions and worked example in R1696 and R1697. The system, though not this letter, is described in Antal, Mírka and Kováč's 2025 study of Viennese obfuscation techniques. Work of 17 and 18 September 2026 by native Codex agents; everything is reproducible from the published files.

Why it is not finished

Nobody on this side has seen the original. The only source is Alexandre Pillon's transcription on Cryptiana, which warns that digits may be missing at the right edge of the image, and several runs of the text do not parse as written. Every place where the reading needs a changed digit is listed below as a proposed repair, never applied to the transcription. The key itself is a provisional reading of eighteenth-century handwriting; where the tables give alternatives, both are kept.

What is established is the system, the two table switches, and long runs of connected German on unchanged digits: the memorandum, the war-or-peace sentence, the matching statements, Minorca, Verlegenheit, Zinner, and the closing advice. What is not established is the opening adjective, four sentence joins, the word before zeige, and the four places where 99 seems to mean g.

Rows 1–6

Opening and the memorandum to Wall · Prima table

German

Cipher, rows 1–6 · Prima

… als ich nun den Innhalt derselben gefunden, so große Hoffnun[g] macht mir … daß … deroselben annoch gelingen werde, die durch das dem Stat[s]secretario Wall behändigte sehr treflich gefasste Memoir so geschickt als vor[sich]ti[g] eingeleitete Handlung nunmehro immer offen zu behalten und den spanischen Hof … zu solchen Resolutionen zu vermögen …

English

Translation

… now that I have seen the contents of the same, it gives me such great hope … that … Your Excellency will yet succeed in keeping open, from now on, the negotiation so skilfully and so cautiously begun through the very well drafted memorandum handed to Secretary of State Wall, and in bringing the Spanish court … to such resolutions …

Notes

  • The row-1 adjective before als ich is unresolved. Removing one surplus 3 permits so / stat / lich, which establishes neither stattlich nor tröstlich.
  • Annoch gelingen werde comes from the uninterrupted an / noch / ge / li / ng / en / werden. Both an (31005) and noch (31019) had been misread in the working key; no target digit is changed.
  • Wall is spelled wa + l here and again in row 19. With the memorandum and the long closing, it supplies three separated anchors for the key match.
  • Stat[s]secretario needs 31168→31368 at offset 195; vor[sich]tig needs the invalid 30073 read as 31073. Both wait on the original.
  • The court passage (row 6) is a damaged join: two deletions yield bi / zu / solch / resolution. Bis zu solchen Resolutionen is a candidate, not an accepted restoration. The baseline output there (nach französisch religion mittelst armee ganz) is what an unrepaired parse produces and is not read as text.

Rows 7–8

War or an honourable peace · Prima, switching to Secunda at 876

German

Cipher, rows 6–8 · Prima → Secunda

… die entweder dem Krieg eine vergnüglichere, als die dermahlige Gestalt ist, geben oder uns ein[en] erspr[ieß]lich[en] … honorablen Frieden verschaffen könnte, und gibt mir zumahl[en] …

English

Translation

… which could either give the war a more agreeable shape than its present one, or procure us a profitable … and honourable peace; and this gives me, moreover, …

Notes

  • Two key corrections, 31036 = Krieg and 31490 = re, recover the first alternative with no digit changes. The literal run is dem / Krieg / ein / ver / gn / u / gl / ich / re / als / die / der / mahl / ig / ge / st / al / t / ist / geben.
  • Frieden is source-supported. The alphabetical F entry reads fried with en and same endings; both numerical copies agree. The earlier reading fremd was wrong and its override has been retired.
  • The join before honorablen sits at the recto/verso boundary. Inserting 31 at 624 and changing 9→3 at 626 gives en / ho / null / n / or / ab / le / n / fried; deleting 119 at 624 is a competing three-edit repair. Neither is authenticated.
  • After the join, ver / [A→B] / schaff / könnte follows unchanged: the table switch 876 falls inside verschaffen.
  • Secunda 53 is mahl (with s/en alternatives), not the earlier meist: the alphabetical entry sits between mach and man. So gi / eb / t / mir / zu / mahl reads gibt mir zumahlen. What follows, von / den / no / ge / n …, is unresolved.

Rows 9–15

The matching statements, Minorca, and Spain's entry · Secunda

German

Cipher, rows 9–15 · Secunda

… Euer Hochgebohrn … französischen Botschafter fast zu gleicher Zeit gemachten, genau gleichförmigen Äusserungen … sehr große Hoffnun[g], daß … Minorca an Spanien überlassen würde, wozu sich Frankreich [bereit?] zeige un[d] wohl leicht [en]tschließen wird, alsdann dieser Hof an dem Krieg mit Theil nehmen und andurch den [engländischen] und preußischen Hof in sehr große Verlegenheit setz[en] würde.

English

Translation

… the exactly matching statements made almost at the same time by Your Excellency … [and] the French ambassador … [give] very great hope that … Minorca would be ceded to Spain, to which France shows itself [ready?] and will surely readily resolve; whereupon this court would take part in the war and thereby put the [English] and Prussian courts in very great embarrassment.

Notes

  • The phrase from französischen Botschafter through Äusserungen uses unchanged digits. Correcting the key readings recovers fa+st, zeit, ge+mach, genau and gl+ei+ch+fo+r+mi+gen+aus+ser+ung. The abbreviated endings are editorial.
  • Minorca an Spanien überlassen würde is unchanged code with grammatical endings expanded. Preuss + n + Hof and ver / le / ge[n] / heit are likewise unchanged.
  • [bereit?] is not established: 31405 reads berg in the historical key.
  • Und wohl leicht entschließen wird needs 31329→31326 at 1046 and 55→95 at 1061. Setzen würde needs the same 55→95 at 1216. One repair supplying en in two unrelated words is economical, and still a conjecture.
  • [Engländischen] needs 31389→31399 at 1139 (Engelland), then an adjectival expansion.
  • The connective before Minorca reads daß / scho / n and is unresolved. Until it is, the passage cannot be read as an unconditional promise of Spanish participation.
  • Row 15 ends with Secunda 897, the period; the closing begins on the same row with Da ich nun.

Rows 16–22

Courier Zinner and the advice to continue · Secunda, back to Prima at 899

German

Cipher, rows 15–22 · Secunda → Prima

Da ich nun Euer Hochgebohrn durch den Courier Zinner zu erstattenden Berichten mit vielem Ver[langen?] ent[gegen]sehe, so brauche indessen nichts be[s]eres einrathen, als in dem bereits eingeschlagenen Weeg fortzufahren und den Wall so viel nur immer möglich durch dero [g]schicktes Benehmen zu weiteren Vertrauenausserungen zu vermögen.

English

Translation

As I await with great eagerness the reports Your Excellency is to send through the courier Zinner, I can advise nothing better in the meantime than to continue along the course already taken and, through your skilful conduct, persuade Wall as far as possible to make further confidential disclosures.

Notes

  • Zinner is 14 zi / 31307 n / 99 er after the whole-word code Courier, with no digit repair. Wille's journal independently records “M. Zinner, courrier impérial” under 5 September 1759 (vol. 1, p. 120). That corroborates a courier of this name, not that the two are the same man.
  • The Zinner occurrence is also what kills a global 99→g: Secunda 99 must stay er at 1298, while four other occurrences (97, 335, 945, 1608) still appear to need g. A target-specific variant or a misread glyph are both possible; nothing settles it without the image.
  • The awaiting clause is the least secure part. 31427 reads lau or lan with a g alternative, so Verlangen is plausible. Ent[gegen]sehe needs an insertion at the row boundary (offset 1369): seven digits 3111531, or only 31 if the longer key alternative for 31115, apparently gege, is right. The two are competing reconstructions.
  • Be[s]eres needs 31168→31368 at 1414. The same code at 525 correctly gives u in vergnüglich, so this repair is selective by design.
  • Nur is explicit in both numerical copies of 31218; weiteren rests on a t alternative in the W entry 31484 that needs no inserted digit. There is no separately encoded zu in einrathen.
  • The final unit is a lone 3 and is incomplete under the recovered rule.

Proposed repairs

None of these is applied to the published transcription. Offsets are zero-based positions in the unchanged 1,721-digit stream. The eight small edits below, together with the two competing closing insertions and the two competing peace-join repairs, combine into eight full-letter parses in which every non-terminal unit parses and both table switches survive. Those parses cost 19 or 24 specified digit edits, not counting the four selective 99→g readings and the initial mark displacement. A valid segmentation is not a solution: several phrases in every candidate remain unreadable.

At offsets 2, 443, 449, 708 and 793, treating the presumed surplus digits as null 8s gives exactly the same code sequence as deleting them, at equal cost. The comparison scans show that 3 and 8 are hard to tell apart in this hand. Parsing cannot decide between the two.

Mechanical verification

Running python3 run_checks.py in the published folder reproduces every figure on this page from the checked-in transcription and keys, with the standard library only. It was rerun on 18 September 2026 before this page was written and left the checkout clean. Passing these checks proves that each conjecture parses as claimed. It does not certify a complete decipherment, and the script says so on its last line.

The two external controls matter most. The chancellery's own worked example was transcribed independently and every unit boundary and its table switch agree with the parser. A separate 1756 Kaunitz letter with surviving plaintext, reproduced in the Antal study, yields two disjoint windows that align on the same key readings: Nachricht, glücklichen, Ankunft, and so den 13 Marti an Euer Excellenz abgefert… with its date codes and table switch. The span between the two windows is not aligned, so they are not one continuous match.

What remains open

In the text

  • The opening adjective in row 1.
  • The join after den spanischen Hof in row 6, where bis zu solchen Resolutionen is only a candidate.
  • The recto/verso join before honorablen Frieden.
  • The sentence leading from gibt mir zumahlen into the matching statements.
  • The connective before Minorca, which decides whether Spain's participation is conditional, expected or promised.
  • Bereit, where the key reads berg.
  • The start of the closing, Verlangen and the length of the entgegensehe insertion.
  • The four 99→g occurrences, and the incomplete terminal 3.

In the sources

  • No image of the letter has been found. Cryptiana links none. The best archival lead is HHStA Frankreich Varia 28-14, five Starhemberg letters to Rosenberg from April to December 1758 catalogued under Spain, Bernis, Minorca, mediation and peace plans, with no item-level listing and no scans.
  • No printed plaintext. Two OCR copies of Arneth's Geschichte Maria Theresia's, volume 5, were searched for the date, Rosenberg, Wall and Minorca without a match; a volume 6 lead from a Bernis footnote turned out to concern 1762. Fraktur OCR limits both negatives.
  • The Antal study reports sixteen parallel letters in the Slovak National Archive (Esterházy Čeklís, box 634). They are not in DECODE and no public transcription was found.
  • The key tables themselves. The working TSVs are provisional readings of the DECODE facsimiles; the gege alternative at 31115 and the g alternative at 31427 in particular need a careful look at the originals.

A scan of the letter would settle most of the first list at once, since nearly every open point is a damaged run of digits rather than a missing key entry.
