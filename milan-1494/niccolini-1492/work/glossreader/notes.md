# c.176r gloss reading notes

Read from the unmasked crops only (plus zooms in `z/`, made with `zoom.py` = autocontrast gray and `zoom2.py` = colour bicubic). The crops are 3x upscales of a 1272 px page scan, so stroke detail is limited. Many letters that look like a known word were left as `?` on purpose: I did not complete words from context. No cipher signs were read or counted.

General: the gloss hand writes long s (rendered `s`), mercantesca-style `a`, and looped `h`/`l`. I made no expansions (`[ ]`). No abbreviation was certain enough to expand.

## B1 (L17) - `As.`
Clear, dark. A capital A, then a long s, then a dot. It sits over the struck-through 2-sign group. The same siglum appears at B5. It is probably an abbreviation (a name?), but I left it unexpanded. Alternative reading of the second letter: `f` (long s and f are hard to tell apart at this resolution; I see no crossbar).

## B2 (L19) - `arr?gha?o`
Dark, but cramped and small. Letters: `a`; two short r-like strokes (could also be `ss` or `n`); a ʒ-shaped letter (`?`), which together with the next letter could be one two-part `g`; a descender letter I read as `g` (could be `y`); a tall looped `h`; `a`; a letter with a descender (`?`: `n`, `p` or `g`); a final `o`. The small `o` under the gloss, over the third cipher sign, is not part of the gloss.

## B3 (L19) - `te`
Clear: a crossed `t`, then `e`. A faint x-like stroke to its right is probably not a letter. Position: it sits right after the clear word ending "...hlm" (before "et da"), over a near-empty space between the two. **It looks more like an insertion/completion of the preceding clear word (e.g. an adverb ending in -te) than a decipherment of a cipher group.** Treat B3 as doubtful as a cipher block.

## B4 (L19) - `??lygnosi ce da sena l?t?n`
- Word 1: the first two letters are a dark cursive pair (`rr`? `ne`? `re`?), then a lighter tall stroke read as `l` (it could be a stray stroke from the line above), then `ygnosi`. The final `i` has a long tail, so `j` is possible.
- Word 2: `ce` (c plus e with a tail flourish). `et` is possible: compare B6, where the same-size word looks like `et`.
- `da sena`: fairly clear. Long s in `sena`.
- Last word: a tall L-shaped first letter with a long baseline tail; then `?` (y/z-like); then `t` (or `h`); then `?`; then `n` (or `in`). Low confidence.
- A short dash (x~2865-2975) follows. It is a line-filler or abbreviation stroke, not a letter. There is no gloss over the tail of the run (x 3000-3450).
